Walls & defence · Updated 27 August 2026

On a double glass, the wall order tells you where to move.

A ball can bounce in your court and then use both the back and side glass before you return it. The difficult part is not the legality; it is learning to predict the exit after the second wall.

Back glass first

When the ball reaches the back wall before the side, the first rebound usually sends it laterally towards the side glass. Give the ball enough space to make the second contact, then move towards the area where it will leave the side.

Side glass first

A ball that clips the side glass first and then reaches the back wall can exit on a different line, often opening more towards the centre of the court. Recognising that first contact early stops you moving to the wrong side of the rebound.

Do not hit between the walls unless you need to

If the ball is clearly travelling towards a playable second rebound, reaching into the corner after the first wall can leave you cramped and late. Let the geometry finish when time allows.

Spacing matters more than racket speed

The ball may change direction twice, so position yourself with room for adjustment. Small steps and a compact preparation give you more chance to respond to spin or a sharper-than-expected exit.

Choose a high-percentage reply

A double-glass defence often starts from a low or late position. A controlled lob, cross-court reset or ball through the middle is usually more useful than trying to manufacture a winner from the corner.

What changes with pace and spin?

A faster ball tends to travel further away from the walls after contact, while heavy slice can keep the rebound lower or make the exit less intuitive. That is why memorising one fixed path is not enough. Read the first wall, create space, then keep adjusting rather than committing your feet too early.

If you are late, prioritise contact and height. Getting the ball safely over the net is more valuable than trying to hit an ambitious target while your body is still moving out of the corner.

How to practise it

Have a partner feed the same corner repeatedly, first back-wall-first and then side-wall-first. Your first goal is simply to call the order before the second contact. Once the read becomes automatic, add the return.

Then randomise the feeds. When you no longer know which wall will come first, you are training the real skill: recognising the path from the incoming ball rather than remembering a rehearsed movement.

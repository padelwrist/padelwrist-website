Social padel formats

Americano scoring explained.

Americano is less about winning one long match and more about collecting points across a series of short rounds with different partners. That is what makes it sociable, fast and slightly chaotic in the best way.

The basic idea

A group of players rotates through a pre-planned set of pairings. In each round, four players share a court and play a fixed number of points rather than a traditional best-of-three match. At the end of the round, each player keeps the number of points their pair won.

Across the session, partners and opponents change. The individual with the highest accumulated points usually finishes top.

Why the score might be 18-14 instead of 6-4

Americano commonly uses a fixed total of points for each round. If a round is set to 32 points, for example, it could finish 18-14, 16-16 or 24-8. Once all 32 points have been played, the round ends.

That is different from normal padel scoring because 15, 30 and 40 are not the main structure. Every rally simply adds one point to one side.

Does every Americano use the same number of points?

No. This is where many online explanations become too definite. Americano is a social format rather than one universal FIP competition rule. Clubs and organisers may use 16, 24, 32 or another number of points per round, depending on court time and group size.

Some organisers also use time-limited rounds, special tie procedures or different rotation tables. Check the event rules before assuming one version is “the” Americano format.

How are winners decided?

Most commonly, players add together the points earned in every round. The highest total wins. An organiser may use point difference, head-to-head results or another method to separate tied players.

Because the ranking is individual, a close loss can still be valuable. Losing 15-17 contributes far more to your total than losing 5-27.

What should you track?

For each round, you need the two pairs, the target or total number of points, and the final points won by each side. If you are organising the whole event, you also need the rotation schedule and cumulative individual table.

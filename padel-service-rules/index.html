Padel rules

Padel service rules: the part new players most often overthink.

The serve in padel is underarm, starts with a bounce and travels diagonally into the opposite service box. Once those three ideas are clear, the rest is mostly positioning and order.

Where does the server stand?

The server starts behind the service line, between the imaginary continuation of the centre service line and the side wall. At least one foot must remain in contact with the ground when the ball is struck.

The server cannot step on the service line or into the receiving box before making contact.

You must bounce the ball first

Unlike tennis, you do not toss the ball overhead. The server bounces it on the ground within the serving area and then hits it at or below waist height.

The serve travels diagonally across the net into the receiver's service box. The first point of a game is served from the right-hand side, then service alternates left and right as points are played.

Do you get a second serve?

Yes. If the first serve is a fault, the server gets a second attempt. That is why keeping track of whether a fault was first or second can matter when correcting a serving-order mistake.

How does serving order work in doubles?

Before each set, each pair decides which player will serve first. Once that order is established, the two players alternate their service games for the rest of the set. The order can be chosen again at the start of the next set.

The receiving pair also establishes its receiving order for the set. In normal play, players do not simply swap sides whenever they feel like it.

What changes in a tie-break?

The player whose turn it is serves the opening point from the right. The opposing team then serves two points, followed by two points from the other side, continuing in the established service order. For the full sequence, see the padel tie-break guide.

Why track the server digitally?

Players usually remember the score before they remember the service rotation. A scorer that keeps the server visible can prevent a correct score being paired with the wrong person serving, especially after a long game or change of ends.

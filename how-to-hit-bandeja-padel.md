# A good bandeja feels controlled because its first job is to keep you in control.

The bandeja is not a weak smash. It is the overhead you use when a lob challenges your net position but does not give you a clean finishing opportunity.

## Start with the purpose

Your goal is normally to play deep enough and low enough that the opponents cannot immediately attack, then recover forward. If you judge the shot only by speed, you will tend to overswing.

## Read the lob and turn early

Late preparation creates most ugly bandejas. As soon as you recognise the lob, turn side-on and move so the ball will be in front and to the side rather than directly above or behind you.

Do not backpedal blindly. Use controlled sideways movement so you can stay balanced.

## Prepare the racket high

A continental-style grip is commonly taught because it supports a stable racket face and slice. Keep the preparation compact and the racket high enough that you are not lifting from underneath the ball at the last second.

## Contact in front, not behind your head

If the ball reaches you while you are falling backwards, the contact becomes difficult to control. Move earlier so you can strike in front of the body with enough space for the racket to travel across and through the ball.

## Think slice and depth, not maximum pace

The classic bandeja uses slice to keep the rebound lower and less comfortable. The exact amount depends on your technique, but the useful cue is that you are shaping a controlled ball rather than hitting a flat full-power overhead.

Deep middle and deep corner targets are usually more useful than trying to find an impossible angle.

## Recover forwards immediately

If the shot was chosen to protect your net position, staying where you hit it defeats the purpose. Once the ball leaves your racket, move forward into the position appropriate to the opponents' likely reply.

## Common bandeja mistakes

** Too much power: ** the shot becomes a risky flat overhead.

** Facing forwards: ** you lose the body position needed for controlled slice and direction.

** Contact too far back: ** usually a movement problem before it is a racket problem.

** Trying to finish the point: ** you choose targets and pace that work against the shot's main purpose.

** Not recovering: ** a decent bandeja becomes tactically useless if you remain stranded behind it.

## When not to play a bandeja

If the lob is short and you are in a strong attacking position, a smash or more aggressive víbora may be appropriate. If the ball is already behind you and you are off balance, retreating and defending may be better than forcing any overhead.

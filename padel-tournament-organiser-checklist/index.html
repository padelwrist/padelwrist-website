Social formats · Organiser guide

A practical checklist for organising a padel event.

Whether you run a weekly club Americano or your first Mexicano social, getting the format and scorekeeping agreed before the first serve is the difference between a smooth event and constant questions.

1. Decide what kind of evening you want

Choose Americano when social mixing and changing partners are the priority. Pairings are usually planned ahead, aiming to distribute partnerships and opponents fairly. Choose Mexicano when you want each new round to reflect how people are performing. After results are confirmed, the next pairing can group players with broadly similar standings.

These are common club conventions rather than one universal set of International Padel Federation tournament rules. Agree the exact variation with your group, especially how you handle tied results and whether people can repeat partners. Read the Americano guide and Mexicano guide before fixing the draw.

2. Match your players to the available courts

Four people play in a doubles match, so eight players on two courts is a convenient simple example. Twelve players can fill three courts and sixteen can fill four if all are available simultaneously. If player numbers exceed court capacity, decide who sits out in each round and make rest distribution visible to everyone.

Don't hide a problem just because the draw looks tidy. If somebody arrives late, withdraws or joins halfway through, write down whether their results stay in the ranking and how any missed rounds will be treated. A fair policy is more important than finding one mathematically perfect schedule.

3. Agree the points target and how a round ends

Many social formats use a fixed combined number of rallies rather than the traditional love, 15, 30 and 40 structure. For example, with a 16-point target, the result could be 10–6 or 8–8. Both pairs' scores must add to 16 if all planned rallies were played.

Each of the two players on the winning side typically earns the team's points toward their individual event total, while both opponents receive their side's points. A drawn fixture should be recorded as a draw, not given a winner arbitrarily. The same approach works with other targets if the organiser chooses them in advance. See Fixed Points scoring explained.

4. Write down your draw and court assignment

For every round, record the four players, their partners and opponents, and the court. If you're running Americano, prepare enough pairings to avoid unnecessary repeated partners. If you're running Mexicano, don't publish several future rounds as if the standings cannot change. Review each subsequent draw after the previous round's results are confirmed.

It helps to display one unambiguous version of the draw. A proposed fixture is not the same as a published fixture; the organiser should be explicit about which matches are live. If a previously confirmed score is corrected, do not silently rearrange a match that players have already started.

5. Make somebody responsible for official scores

Agree who records a score, whether both teams check it, and how someone can challenge a mistake. If you are keeping individual points totals, confirm each fixture exactly once so that a correction replaces its previous value rather than adding the points a second time.

Keep provisional standings clearly labelled. When the final round is finished, identify any incomplete matches and resolve them before announcing a winner. Decide the ranking tie-break order before the event, including how equal total points, wins or point difference will be treated.

6. Leave room for the unexpected

Even a carefully prepared session can have a late player, a court becoming unavailable or a round that runs long. Flexible rounds let you stop when court time runs out, rather than forcing everybody into a schedule you cannot finish. If people played unequal numbers of fixtures, don't quietly compare their raw totals as though everyone had the same opportunities.

A simple session checklist is: roster confirmed, courts labelled, scoring target agreed, rotation method explained, byes and substitutions covered, round status clear, score approver nominated, standings rules visible and finish time agreed.

What can PadelWrist do for your event?

Today, PadelWrist scores individual matches, including Fixed Points, on Apple Watch, iPhone and iPad. It doesn't yet generate complete event draws, assign all tournament courts or publish tournament standings.

PadelWrist Events is in development as a native iPhone/iPad organiser experience focused on those broader tasks. Proposed features include organiser-approved results, flexible rounds and individual rankings. The finer rules for withdrawals, rest compensation and corrections are still being tested. This is a product preview, not an available organiser service.

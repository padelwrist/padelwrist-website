Mexicano scoring explained.

Mexicano starts a little like Americano, then lets the table shape what happens next. Stronger performers tend to be grouped together as the session develops, which usually creates progressively closer rounds.

The main difference from Americano

In a classic Americano, the rotation is usually planned in advance so players meet a broad mix of partners and opponents. In Mexicano, later pairings are commonly based on the current ranking or points table.

That means your next court and pairing may depend on how well you have performed so far rather than on a fixed schedule created before the first round.

How is a round scored?

Like Americano, Mexicano is often played as a fixed number of rally points rather than traditional 15, 30, 40 games. If the organiser chooses 24 points, the round continues until 24 points have been played and might finish 14-10 or 12-12.

Players normally add their pair's points from each round to their own individual total.

How are the next pairings decided?

A common approach is to sort players by their cumulative score after each round, then create new groups from players who are close together in the table. The precise pairing algorithm varies by organiser or software.

That variation matters. There is no single universal Mexicano draw method that every club follows, so the event rules should explain how ties, odd numbers and repeat partnerships are handled.

Why people like Mexicano

The ranking-driven structure can make later rounds more competitive. If the first round exposes a large skill difference, players are more likely to move towards opponents performing at a similar level as the session continues.

It also means every point matters. A pair can lose the round but still protect their individual position by keeping the score close.

What does a scorer need to do?

For an individual round, scoring is straightforward: one rally equals one point. For the whole Mexicano session, the harder part is recalculating standings and generating the next set of pairings.

PadelWrist's Fixed Points mode can score an individual point-based match, but it does not currently run a Mexicano event, create the next round or maintain tournament standings.

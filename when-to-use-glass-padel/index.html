Walls & defence · Updated 18 August 2026

The glass is not something you survive. It is something you use.

One of the biggest changes for a new padel player is learning that a deep ball can become easier after it rebounds. The decision is not “wall or no wall”; it is whether the rebound will create a better contact point than taking the ball early.

Let it hit the glass when the ball is deep and carrying pace

A ball that lands deep near the back of the court and continues towards the wall will often rebound into space. If you rush forwards and hit it before the glass, you may be taking the ball close to your feet or while moving backwards.

Give yourself room behind the bounce and let the court return the ball towards you.

Take it before the glass when the rebound will die

Not every deep-looking ball will come back far enough to play comfortably. A softer ball can reach the wall with very little energy and drop almost underneath it. If you can take that ball cleanly before the glass without being rushed, that may be the better choice.

Watch the bounce, not just the ball speed

The useful information is the combination of depth, height and pace after the bounce. A hard ball that lands short may lose energy before the wall. A medium-paced ball landing very deep may create an excellent rebound.

With experience, you stop deciding at the last moment. You recognise the likely rebound from the bounce location.

Create space before the wall contact

The common beginner problem is standing too close to the back glass. If the ball rebounds into your body, the wall appears unpredictable when the real issue is spacing.

Move so that the rebound comes into your hitting zone. Your feet should solve the problem before your racket does.

Side glass decisions are about angle

When a ball travels towards the side wall after bouncing, the rebound can open or close the angle depending on where it lands. Do not chase the original line of the ball. Anticipate where it will leave the glass and move towards that space.

Corner rebounds add another layer because the ball can contact two surfaces. See our corner glass guide for that situation.

Can the ball hit your own glass before you send it over?

Yes, in normal play you may return the ball off your own glass so that it then crosses the net into the opponents' court. Your own metal fence is different. For the complete legal distinctions, use the padel wall rules.

The practical beginner rule

If a ball is deep, fast enough to rebound and awkward to take early, your first thought should be: can the glass give me more time? If the ball is dying, comfortably reachable before the wall or likely to rebound poorly, take it earlier.

The aim is not to use the wall as often as possible. It is to choose the easier version of the shot.

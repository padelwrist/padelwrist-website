Rules · Reviewed 18 August 2026

Padel rules, without the rulebook fog.

You do not need to memorise every competition regulation to play a proper match. These are the rules that actually come up on court, with links to deeper explanations where the detail matters.

Primary source: International Padel Federation Rules of Padel.

The five rules to know first

1. The serve is underarm, after a bounce, and travels diagonally into the opposite service box.

2. During a rally the ball may bounce once on your side. After that bounce, your own glass or fence may be used before you return it.

3. Your shot must cross the net and bounce in the opponents' court before touching their wall or fence.

4. The return of serve must bounce. After that, volleys are allowed.

5. The basic score is tennis-style, but deuce rules can vary.

The serve

Stand behind the service line, bounce the ball in your own service area and strike it underarm below waist height. Serve diagonally into the opposite service box. The server starts from the right side at the beginning of a game and alternates side after each point.

The serve is a fault if it misses the correct box. If it bounces correctly and then hits the metal fence, it is also a fault. A legal bounce followed by the glass is allowed.

Read padel service rules for foot position, lets and serving order.

Using the walls

If the incoming ball bounces in your court first, it can then hit the back or side wall and remain live. You may hit it after that rebound, provided it has not bounced on the floor a second time.

You can also deliberately play the ball off your own glass to send it back across the net. What you cannot do is hit the ball directly into the opponents' wall or fence before it lands in their court.

Our wall rules guide covers the awkward examples.

Volleys and the return of serve

Volleys are legal during a rally. The major exception is the receiver's first shot: the serve must bounce in the service box before being returned. Once the return is made, both pairs can volley normally.

When do you lose the point?

You lose the point if the ball bounces twice on your side, you hit it into the net and it does not cross, you send it out without a legal rebound, or your shot hits the opponents' wall or fence before touching their court. Touching the net with your body or racket while the point is live is also a fault.

Scoring

Standard points are love, 15, 30 and 40. Games build a set, normally to six with a two-game lead, and a tie-break is normally played at 6-6. Matches are commonly best of three sets.

The deuce system depends on the competition or agreed format. Traditional advantage, deciding-point systems and the FIP's 2026 Star Point are distinct formats. See how padel scoring works in 2026.

Lets

A serve touching the net and then landing legally may be replayed as a let. There are other situations where a point may be replayed under official rules, but for normal club play the net-cord serve is the one you are most likely to encounter.

Can you leave the court to play a ball?

In courts built and authorised for out-of-court play, a player can leave through the opening to retrieve certain balls that have bounced out. This spectacular rule is real, but it is not available on every court because the required safety and access area must exist.

2026 rule context

The FIP introduced Star Point across specified international circuits from 2026. That does not mean every social match or domestic league automatically uses it. Always follow the rules published by the event or organiser you are playing in.

Court basics · Reviewed 27 August 2026

Padel court dimensions.

A standard padel court is 20 metres long and 10 metres wide. The important bit is not only the footprint: the enclosure is part of the sport.

The official footprint

The playing area is a rectangle measuring 20m by 10m, divided in half by the net. Each half is therefore 10m by 10m. Service lines and the centre service line create the boxes used for the underarm serve.

Why the walls matter

The back and sides are enclosed by a combination of glass or solid wall and metal mesh. Once the ball has bounced on the floor, rebounds from the enclosure can remain live. That is why a padel court is not simply a small tennis court with fencing around it.

The net

The net divides the court across the 10m width. Its official height varies slightly between the centre and posts. For players, the practical effect is familiar: the middle is the lowest part, so controlled balls through the centre are often safer than forcing sharp angles.

Service boxes

The service line sits 3m from the back wall. A centre line divides the area between the service line and net into left and right service boxes. The serve travels diagonally into the opposite box.

Why padel feels compact

Four players share 200 square metres of playing surface. Compared with tennis, the shorter distances and enclosing walls create more repeated close-range exchanges. Positioning as a pair matters more than simply being fast enough to reach the ball.

The dimensions also explain why the lob is so important. A good lob forces opponents to retreat only a few metres, but that movement is enough to change who controls the net. At the back, players can let suitable balls travel beyond them and use the glass rather than trying to intercept everything before the bounce.

Can courts differ?

Commercial courts can differ in materials, lighting, roof clearance and access design while still conforming to the playing dimensions. Some courts also permit authorised out-of-court play through the side openings, provided the required safety area exists.

What beginners should notice

On your first visit, look at the distance from the service line to the back glass and how little space exists between partners when both defend correctly. Those references help positioning make sense much faster than memorising measurements alone.

Source

Dimensions are based on the current International Padel Federation Rules of Padel. Venue construction specifications should always be checked against the full current technical requirements.

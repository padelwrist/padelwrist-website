# A better padel serve gives you an easier next ball.

The serve does not need to be a winner. Its job is to start the rally on your terms, give you time to move forward and make the receiver play a return that you and your partner can handle.

## Start with legality and repeatability

Before trying to add more pace or spin, make sure the basic action is reliable. The ball must be bounced in the serving area and struck at or below waist height into the diagonal service box. A serve that is technically impressive but regularly misses the box is not helping your service game.

If the rules themselves are unclear, start with the [ padel service rules ](https://padelwrist.com/padel-service-rules/) .

## Depth matters more than maximum speed

A serve that lands deep in the box gives the receiver less room to step forward and can make the glass more relevant. A faster serve that lands short often gives the opponent a comfortable contact point and extra time to attack.

Think about where the second bounce would travel if the receiver left the ball alone. Depth that pushes the returner backwards is usually more useful than speed that simply reaches them earlier.

## Choose a target before you bounce the ball

Serving with a clear target makes your action more repeatable. Common options include the side glass, the body or the middle. You do not need to hit the line. Give yourself enough margin to make the first serve regularly.

Variation becomes more valuable once the basic locations are reliable. If every serve goes to the glass, a receiver can start setting up for it before you swing.

## Add slice without turning the serve into a trick shot

A small amount of slice or sidespin can make the bounce and glass rebound less comfortable. The useful spin comes from a controlled racket path and face angle, not a last-second flick of the wrist.

Too much slice can cost you pace and depth. If the ball floats short, simplify the action and prioritise a clean strike through the target.

## Use less effort than you think

Many recreational players serve harder when they want a better serve. That often creates exactly the opposite result: a bigger swing, later contact and fewer first serves in.

Try building a serve you could repeat ten times with the same rhythm. Once the direction and depth are stable, increase pace gradually without changing the basic shape of the movement.

## Make the second serve look like the first

A second serve that becomes a slow push tells the receiver they can step forward and attack. You still need safety, but try to preserve the same preparation and target idea while taking enough pace off to increase margin.

The goal is not to prove that the second serve is aggressive. It is to avoid handing the receiver a completely different, easier ball.

## Serve with the next shot in mind

Your serve and first volley are one sequence. A well-placed serve should give you time to move forward and make the return more predictable. If you serve wide to the glass, think about the likely return angle before you start moving.

This is why serving harder is not automatically better. A serve you can place reliably makes your next decision easier.

## A simple practice progression

Start with ten serves to one large target and record how many land deep enough. Then repeat to a second target. Only after you can control location should you add more slice or pace.

A useful session is ** placement first, depth second, spin third, speed last ** . That order protects the part of the serve that matters most in a real match: consistency.

## Common serve mistakes

** Chasing aces: ** the serve is usually a setup shot rather than a finishing shot.

** Serving too short: ** the receiver gets to step into the return.

** Overusing wrist: ** contact becomes difficult to repeat.

** No target: ** you hit into the box instead of serving with a tactical purpose.

** Watching the serve: ** you delay the move towards your first-volley position.

## Build the whole service pattern.

Once the serve itself is reliable, learn [ what to do immediately after serving ](https://padelwrist.com/what-to-do-after-serving-padel/) and how the first volley connects to net position.

Getting started · Reviewed 18 August 2026

What is padel?

Padel is a racket sport usually played by four people on an enclosed court. It borrows tennis-style scoring, adds glass walls that stay in play, and uses an underarm serve. The result is a sport that is quick to pick up but much more tactical than it first appears.

Padel in 30 seconds

Two pairs play across a net on a 20m by 10m enclosed court. You can volley the ball or let it bounce once. After the ball bounces in your court, it may rebound off the glass or fence and still be playable. Points are normally scored 15, 30, 40 and game.

Why are there walls?

The walls are not just barriers. They are part of the rally after the ball has bounced on the floor. A ball that looks as though it has beaten you can rebound off the back glass and give you time to play it. Learning to stop panicking when the ball reaches the glass is one of the first big breakthroughs for a new player.

Read how the walls work in padel for the situations that cause the most confusion.

How is padel different from tennis?

Padel uses a solid perforated racket rather than a strung one, a smaller enclosed court and an underarm serve. It is predominantly a doubles sport. The scoring is familiar to tennis players, but the walls and net positioning completely change how rallies develop.

Power matters less than many beginners expect. Keeping the ball low, controlling the net and choosing when to lob are often more useful than trying to hit winners.

How do you serve?

The serve begins behind the service line. The server bounces the ball and strikes it underarm, below waist height, diagonally into the opposite service box. You get a first and second serve. A legal serve can rebound from the glass after its first bounce, but a serve that bounces in the box and then hits the metal fence is a fault.

How does scoring work?

The usual sequence is love, 15, 30, 40 and game. Sets are normally played to six games with a two-game margin, with a tie-break commonly used at 6-6. What happens at 40-40 depends on the scoring system being used.

What equipment do you need?

For a first game, you need a padel racket, padel balls, suitable court shoes and ordinary sports clothing. Most clubs can hire rackets, so buying one before your first session is unnecessary. Shoes with reliable lateral grip matter more than looking the part.

Is padel easy to learn?

It is relatively easy to start a rally because the serve is gentler than tennis and the smaller court reduces the amount of ground you cover. The difficult part comes later: learning when to move forward, how to defend the glass, when to lob and how to play as a pair rather than two individuals.

What should a beginner learn first?

Learn the serve, the basic score, the one-bounce rule and what happens when the ball reaches the back glass. Then focus on returning the ball rather than hitting hard. A simple rally with good positioning teaches more than a sequence of attempted winners.

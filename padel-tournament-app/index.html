What does a padel tournament organiser actually need?

Organising a social is more than keeping a single match score. You need an accurate player roster, available courts, a scoring target, a pairing method, round-by-round results and a leaderboard people can trust. PadelWrist Events brings those parts together in a native iPhone and iPad experience.

It is designed for clubs, coaches, regular social groups and independent hosts running point-based Americano and Mexicano formats. It is not a court-booking, entry-payment or club-membership platform.

The event journey: setup, draw, scores, results

  1. Set the event. Name the social, choose Americano or Mexicano, enter the players and courts and agree a target number of rally points per match.
  2. Review the draw. The app prepares proposed partnerships, opponents and court assignments. The organiser decides when to publish a round.
  3. Keep everyone informed. Participants follow published fixtures, courts, results and standings by code or link without gaining editing access.
  4. Record official results. The organiser enters the score for each fixture, can make tracked corrections and keeps control of the authoritative event.
  5. Finish with clarity. Review the individual standings and final podium, then find the completed event and its recorded times in event history.

Americano or Mexicano: which is right for your group?

Americano is usually the more sociable rotation format. Pairings are planned ahead to mix partners and opponents, with each player's points contributing to their individual total. Mexicano adapts later pairings to the standings as the event develops. It can bring players with similar performances together for increasingly competitive rounds.

Neither format has one universal social-club rulebook. Before you begin, agree how many points to play, how to handle ties and what happens to players resting when there are more participants than court places. Read our format comparison for the differences.

What happens if someone is resting or leaves early?

Real club evenings rarely run exactly to plan. PadelWrist 2.0 includes organiser controls for resting players, withdrawals and ending an event early. Resting-point settings should be chosen deliberately: an organiser can use a personal-average rest credit or award no points, depending on the event's agreed rules. Describe your policy to players before publishing the first round.

Corrections to completed matches can affect standings. Official scoring and ranking remain under organiser control rather than letting every participant edit a result.

Can players see their next match?

Yes, in version 2.0's read-only event following experience. A shared event code or link lets participants check their partner, opponents and court when the organiser publishes a round, then follow results and individual standings. The organising device remains the source of truth, with local-first event management designed to cope with intermittent connectivity.

Event sharing is not the same thing as live sharing of an individual padel match. PadelWrist supports both experiences for different needs.

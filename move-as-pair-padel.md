# How to Move as a Pair in Padel | PadelWrist

# Your partner's position changes where you should stand.

Padel doubles works best when the pair attacks, defends and shifts together. The exact distance between players changes, but the principle is simple: do not leave one person solving a different rally from the other.

## Think of an elastic line between you

If the ball moves wide towards your partner, both of you shift towards that side. The player nearest the ball protects the line and direct angle; the partner moves across to protect the middle. When the ball switches sides, the pair shifts again.

## Move forward together

A successful deep lob can force the opponents away from the net. That is the cue for both partners to advance. If only one goes forward, the opponents can play into the large gap between the net player and the partner still defending.

## Retreat together when the net is lost

If a lob beats one player cleanly, the other should recognise that the pair's attacking shape has changed. Trying to defend the net alone often leaves easy angles and makes the retreating partner cover too much court.

## Avoid camping in mid-court

The transition area is somewhere you move through. If both players stop halfway, they are vulnerable to balls at their feet and lobs over their heads. Commit to the attacking or defensive phase as a pair.

## Who covers the middle?

Both players have responsibility for it, but not equally on every ball. Handedness, forehand/backhand side and who has the stronger attacking contact affect the decision. Agree simple priorities before the point rather than discovering them with both rackets swinging.

## Communication makes movement earlier

Useful calls such as “back”, “stay”, “mine”, “yours” and information about opponents' positions allow your partner to move before they can see everything themselves. Good communication is positional information, not constant commentary.

## Practise the shape without keeping score

Try a cooperative drill where the only goal is to keep both partners in the same phase. Feed lobs, low balls and wide balls, then stop the rally whenever one player is at the net and the other is stranded deep without a tactical reason. Removing the score makes the spacing problem obvious very quickly.

## The visual check

After every shot, ask two questions: where is the ball going, and where is my partner? If you are several metres ahead, behind or across from the shape they are defending, there should be a tactical reason for it.

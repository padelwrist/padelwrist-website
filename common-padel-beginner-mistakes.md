# Most beginner padel mistakes are really the same three mistakes in disguise.

New players usually rush the ball, carry tennis habits into a smaller court, or try to finish points before they have earned an attacking position. Fix those patterns and a surprising number of errors disappear together.

## 1. Rushing the back glass

A deep ball is not automatically a ball you need to hit before it reaches the wall. If it has enough depth and pace, letting it bounce off the back glass can give you more time and a more comfortable contact point.

Beginners often step forwards and snatch at these balls because the rebound feels unfamiliar. The better habit is to read the trajectory early, give yourself room behind the bounce and decide whether the glass will help you.

Read [ when to let the ball hit the glass ](https://padelwrist.com/when-to-use-glass-padel/) and the [ padel wall rules ](https://padelwrist.com/padel-wall-rules/) .

## 2. Staying at the back after a good opportunity

The back of the court is a useful defensive position, not a permanent home. When you play a good lob that pushes both opponents behind you, that is usually your invitation to move forward together and take the net.

Many beginners hit the right shot and then watch it. In doubles padel, your recovery position is part of the shot.

## 3. One player goes forward and the other stays back

A split pair leaves a large diagonal gap and gives opponents easy choices. You do not need to stand shoulder-to-shoulder, but you usually want to defend or attack as a connected pair.

If one partner is forced back by a lob, the other normally needs to recognise the change and move with them rather than guarding an empty net alone.

## 4. Hitting every volley as hard as possible

At the net, placement and depth usually matter more than maximum pace. A hard volley that rebounds comfortably off the back wall may simply give your opponents time and energy to counterattack.

Think about keeping the ball low, using the middle and making the next ball awkward. The aim of many volleys is to preserve pressure rather than end the rally immediately.

## 5. Smashing every lob

A high ball is not automatically a smash. If the lob is deep and pushes you backwards, an attempted winner can turn a strong net position into a scramble.

The bandeja exists for exactly this problem: deal with the lob, keep control and recover forward. Save the aggressive smash for the ball that actually gives you the position and contact point to attack.

## 6. Playing down the line by default

Down-the-line shots have their place, but beginners often choose them simply because they look direct. Cross-court usually gives you more court length and more margin. The middle also removes angle from the opponents.

Choose direction because of opponent position, not because the line looks tempting.

## 7. Lobbing only when desperate

The lob is not just an emergency shot. It is one of the main ways a defending pair changes the geometry of the rally and challenges the opponents' net position.

A balanced lob from the back can be more attacking than a hard drive because it forces the net pair to retreat or play an overhead.

## 8. Forgetting the score, server or order

Score disputes are rarely dramatic; they usually start with four people concentrating on the rally and nobody saying the score clearly. Call the score, know who is serving and agree the format before the first point.

This is also the problem PadelWrist is designed to reduce: score, server and match state should be obvious without becoming another distraction.

## What should a beginner fix first?

Do not try to rebuild everything in one session. Start with one positional habit and one shot-selection habit. A good first combination is: ** use the glass instead of rushing deep balls ** , and ** move forward with your partner after a successful lob ** .

Those changes improve your decision-making before you have learnt any spectacular new shots.

# How to Return Serve in Padel: Position & Shot Choice | PadelWrist

# Your first job on return is to stop the server getting an easy first volley.

The padel return is normally played from a defensive position while the serving pair wants to move forward. A reliable return gives you time, keeps the ball low or pushes the server back before the rally develops.

## Where should you stand?

Start far enough behind the service line to react to depth and leave space for a serve that reaches the back glass, but not so deep that every return becomes a stretched defensive shot. Adjust for serve pace, bounce and whether the server is using the glass well.

## Let deep serves reach the glass when it helps

A good serve into the corner can be awkward to take immediately after the bounce. If it has enough depth and pace, allowing the glass to return it can create a cleaner contact.

If this is the return that keeps beating you, use the dedicated guide to [ returning a padel serve off the glass ](https://padelwrist.com/return-padel-serve-off-glass/) . It covers spacing, early preparation and deciding whether to take the ball before or after the rebound.

## Keep the swing compact

You are often receiving a low ball with limited preparation time. A large tennis-style backswing makes timing harder. Use a shorter movement and prioritise direction and height over raw pace.

## Drive low when you can make the first volley awkward

A controlled return towards the server's feet or through the middle can force the serving pair to volley upwards. That buys your team time and may prevent them establishing an easy attacking rhythm.

## Lob when you have enough control

A good return lob can immediately challenge the serving pair's planned move to the net. The risk is the short lob: if you are off balance and leave it around the service line, you may simply hand over an easy overhead.

## Return cross-court or down the line?

Cross-court gives more court length and often more margin. Through the middle reduces angle and can create uncertainty. Down the line can be effective when the server leaves space, but it offers less margin over the higher part of the net near the side.

## Do not try to win the return by default

The serving team begins the rally with a structural plan: serve, move forward, volley. Your return should disrupt that plan. A low, deep or well-lobbed ball is successful even if it is not spectacular.

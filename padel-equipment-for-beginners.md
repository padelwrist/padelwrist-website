# Padel equipment for beginners.

You need less than the shops would like you to believe. For a first session, hire a racket, wear proper court shoes and spend your money only after you know you enjoy the sport.

## What you actually need

A padel racket, suitable balls, court shoes and comfortable sports clothing. Most clubs provide the balls and offer rental rackets, which means shoes are usually the only thing worth thinking about before your first booking.

## Padel racket

Padel rackets are solid and perforated rather than strung. Beginner rackets are often rounder with a forgiving sweet spot and manageable balance. Do not buy an advanced head-heavy racket because somebody on YouTube hits spectacular smashes with one.

Hire a racket for your first few sessions if the venue allows it. Once you can tell what feels too heavy, too stiff or too lively, buying becomes much easier.

## Padel balls

They look similar to tennis balls but are made to padel specifications. If the club supplies balls, use theirs. Old balls lose pressure and change how high they rebound from the glass, so a dead set can make the sport feel strangely slow.

## Shoes

Lateral grip matters. Running shoes are designed mainly for forward movement and can feel unstable during repeated sideways changes of direction. Padel or suitable tennis/court shoes give better support for the movement the sport actually requires.

## Clothing

Normal breathable sports clothing is fine. You do not need padel-specific shorts or shirts. Pockets are useful because the server normally needs to carry the second ball.

## Overgrips

An overgrip wraps around the handle to adjust feel, absorb sweat and slightly change the grip size. They are cheap, replaceable and one of the few pieces of kit players genuinely go through regularly.

## Wrist strap

Padel rackets include a safety cord or strap. Use it. It is not decoration: it helps stop the racket leaving your hand during play.

## What not to buy yet

Do not rush into premium rackets, specialist bags, cases of balls or accessories before you know how often you will play. A few sessions will tell you far more about your preferences than a product description can.

If you do want to spend money early, prioritise comfort rather than performance claims. Shoes that feel secure and an inexpensive overgrip can improve a session immediately. A £300 racket will not fix late preparation, poor positioning or inconsistent contact, and beginners often change their racket preferences quickly once they start playing regularly.

Serve rules · Reviewed 29 September 2026

Padel let rules without the mid-match debate.

A serve touching the net is not automatically good, bad or replayed. You still have to look at where the ball lands and what it touches next.

Reviewed against Rules 7, 9 and 10 of the current International Padel Federation Rules of Padel.

The easiest way to judge a net-cord serve

Temporarily ignore the fact that the ball touched the net and ask one question: would the serve otherwise have been legal?

If the answer is yes, the net touch normally makes it a let and the serve is repeated. If the serve would still have been a fault because of where it bounced or what it touched afterwards, the net does not rescue it.

Net, correct box, then glass: let

If the serve clips the net, lands in the correct diagonal service box and then behaves like an otherwise legal serve, it is replayed. A ball that bounces correctly and then reaches the glass can still be a legal serve, so a net touch before that sequence produces a let rather than a fault.

Net, correct box, then fence: fault

This is the situation that causes a lot of arguments. A normal serve that lands in the correct box and then touches the metallic fence before its second bounce is a fault. Therefore, if the ball clips the net first, lands correctly and then hits the fence, it is still a fault.

The net touch does not override the fence rule. Judge what would have happened without the net cord.

What happens after a first-serve let?

Under the current FIP rules, a let on the first serve repeats the complete point. The server again has two serves available.

That is useful to remember because some players assume the let simply consumes the first attempt. It does not. A legal first-serve let resets the service sequence.

What happens after a second-serve let?

If the net or let happens on the second serve, only the second serve is repeated. The server does not regain a new first serve.

There is no special two-let limit written into the rule. If another genuine let occurs on the repeated second serve, the second serve is replayed again.

Receiver not ready

A serve can also be replayed when the receiver was not ready, provided the situation falls within the rule and the receiver has not simply attempted to play the ball anyway. In organised competition, the umpire decides whether a let should be awarded.

Lets during a rally

Not every let is a service let. The rules also allow a point to be replayed for unexpected situations unrelated to the players, such as a ball splitting or an outside object entering the playing area. Interference can also produce a let when it is involuntary.

In casual padel, the practical principle is safety and fairness. If an unexpected ball rolls onto court, stop rather than trying to finish the rally around it.

A quick decision guide

Serve clips net + lands legally + no fence before second bounce: let, replay the serve.

Serve clips net + lands outside the correct box: fault.

Serve clips net + lands in + hits metallic fence before second bounce: fault.

First-serve let: repeat the point with two serves available.

Second-serve let: repeat the second serve only.

Why this matters for scorekeeping

A let does not award a point, so the score should stay exactly where it was. That sounds obvious until four players are trying to remember whether the previous rally had already changed the score.

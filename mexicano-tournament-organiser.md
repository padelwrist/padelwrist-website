# How to run a Mexicano padel tournament.

Mexicano changes as the scores come in. Instead of fixing every pairing before play, later rounds use the confirmed standings to suggest who should play together next.

## 1. Start with the people, courts and scoring target

As with an Americano, each doubles match takes four players. Count how many fixtures you can run at once, then decide how many rounds fit your booking. For example, 12 players can fill three courts, while 14 players using three courts need a rest rotation.

Mexicano is often played using a fixed number of rallies per match. With a 24-point target, scores might finish 13–11, 16–8 or 12–12. Each player typically earns their pair's points towards an individual total. Clubs can agree other targets and variations.

## 2. Understand why a Mexicano draw changes

Americano generally uses a planned rotation to mix partnerships. Mexicano typically uses the current individual rankings to make later-round pairings. As results accumulate, players performing at a similar level may increasingly share courts, partners or opposition.

The precise pairing rules vary between clubs and apps. There is no universal Mexicano algorithm. When several players are level, organisers should know how the event handles ties and repeated partnerships. Avoid promising that every draw will always be perfectly balanced.

## 3. Publish only after the preceding scores are confirmed

Round-one pairings can be prepared before the event begins. Later pairings depend on recorded results. An incorrect score may affect who should play next, so confirm official scores before generating the following draw.

In PadelWrist 2.0, the organiser reviews each proposed pairing and court assignment, then chooses when to publish the round. It is not an uncontrolled automatic reshuffle of fixtures already shared with the group.

## 4. Handle resting players transparently

When more players attend than the available courts can hold, people will sit out. PadelWrist Events allows organisers to choose personal-average rest points or no rest points for the event. Explain the rule at the beginning because it affects cumulative totals and therefore can influence later Mexicano draws.

Also decide in advance how withdrawals, interrupted matches or early finishes will be treated. A fair Mexicano needs trustworthy standings as much as it needs clever pairings.

## 5. Keep scores, standings and courts visible

After the organiser records match results, the individual table reflects the confirmed scores. Share published pairings and court assignments by event code or link so players can check their next match in read-only mode. The organising iPhone or iPad remains responsible for official changes.

When the final round has been completed, review the standings and podium and retain the event in history. If scores need correcting, handle the change through the organiser rather than quietly adjusting a shared spreadsheet.

## Quick Mexicano organiser checklist

- Player list, available courts and planned number of rounds

- Fixed rally-point target and treatment of tied results

- Clear rest-point and withdrawal rules

- Agreed handling of equal ranks and repeat partners

- Official score confirmation before the next draw

- One shared source for published courts, results and standings

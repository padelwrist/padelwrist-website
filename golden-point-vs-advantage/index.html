Deuce formats explained

Golden point or advantage? Same game, very different 40-40.

Both formats use the normal 15, 30, 40 score. The difference appears at deuce, and it can completely change the feel and length of a close game.

Advantage scoring

With traditional advantage scoring, 40-40 becomes deuce. The next point gives one pair advantage. If that pair wins again, the game is over. If they lose the next point, the score returns to deuce.

There is no fixed limit to how many times a game can return to deuce. A tight game can therefore last considerably longer than four or five points.

Golden point scoring

Golden point is the no-advantage version. At 40-40, the next rally decides the game. Under the current FIP rule, the receiving pair chooses whether to receive on the right or left side. The receiving players do not swap positions just for that deciding point.

It is simple, quick and high-pressure: one rally settles a game that would otherwise move into advantage.

Which is better?

There is no universal answer. Advantage rewards the ability to create and then convert a two-point cushion. It can feel more traditional and gives a pair a chance to recover after losing the first point at deuce. Golden point makes match length more predictable and gives 40-40 a sudden-death feel.

For a casual booking with a hard finish time, golden point can be useful. For players who enjoy longer tactical games and repeated deuce battles, advantage often feels more natural.

And what is Star Point?

The 2026 FIP rules add a third option called Star Point. It keeps advantage play for a limited number of deuce cycles, then introduces a deciding point at the third deuce. In other words, it sits between unlimited advantage and immediate golden point.

That matters because “official padel scoring” no longer means there is only one possible deuce format. The format should be agreed before the match or defined by the competition.

A practical example

At 40-40 under golden point, Team One wins the next rally and wins the game immediately. Under advantage, that same rally only gives Team One advantage. They must win the following rally as well; if Team Two wins it, the score returns to deuce.

Serve & return · Updated 29 September 2026

Stop fighting the glass on return.

A low serve into the corner feels horrible when you are new to padel. The fix is rarely a bigger swing. It is better spacing, earlier preparation and deciding sooner whether the glass is helping you or trapping you.

First: remember the return rule

You must let the serve bounce in the service box before returning it. After that bounce, the ball can reach the glass and remain live. You then have to play it before a second ground bounce.

That means the glass is not something you have failed to avoid. It is often part of the return you are supposed to use.

Why the corner serve feels so difficult

The server is trying to combine three things: depth, low bounce and angle. When the ball reaches the side or back glass, its direction changes just as you are already under time pressure.

Beginners often make the problem worse by standing too close to the glass, taking a large backswing or moving their whole body towards the ball until there is no room left to swing. The result is usually a rushed contact at knee height.

Create space before the ball arrives

Do not glue yourself to the side glass to protect it. Leave enough room for the rebound to come out towards you. Start balanced, stay low and make small adjustment steps as you read the serve.

Your position should let you move either forward to take the ball before the glass or slightly away from the wall to receive the rebound. If you begin trapped against the glass, both options disappear.

Prepare the racket early and keep the swing small

A fast serve already supplies pace. You do not need to manufacture another full groundstroke. Get the racket prepared early, keep it in front of you and use a short, controlled action.

Think of the return as guiding or blocking the ball rather than trying to hit through it. The more rushed you are, the smaller the swing should become.

When should you let it hit the glass?

Let the glass help when the serve is deep enough that taking it immediately after the bounce would leave you cramped, stretched or making contact too close to the floor. A clean rebound can slow the ball and create a more comfortable contact point.

The important part is not to wait passively. Move with the expected rebound, stay low and prepare before the ball changes direction.

When should you take it before the glass?

If the serve is slower, sits up or gives you comfortable space after the bounce, taking it before the glass can remove uncertainty and give the serving pair less time to establish the net.

Do not turn that into a rule that every serve must be taken early. If you have to reach, swipe or jam your body into the corner, the glass probably offered the better option.

What should you do with the return?

The goal is normally to neutralise the serving pair rather than hit a winner. A compact return towards the server's feet can make their first volley difficult. Through the middle reduces angles. A controlled lob can push the pair away from the net if you have enough time and balance to execute it.

Against a fast serve, borrowing the incoming pace and returning low is often safer than trying to accelerate. Against a slower serve, a well-shaped lob can immediately change the geometry of the point.

A simple practice drill

Ask a partner to serve repeatedly into the same glass corner for ten minutes. For the first few minutes, return every ball after the glass. Then return every ball before it reaches the glass when possible. Finally, mix the two and decide based on depth and pace.

This removes the match pressure and gives you the repetition needed to learn the rebound. In a normal game you might only see the difficult version a few times, which makes it much slower to build confidence.

Three cues to remember

Space: do not crowd the glass.

Prepare: racket ready before the rebound.

Compact: guide the return rather than taking a full swing.

Once those become automatic, the serve into the corner stops feeling like an emergency and starts becoming a predictable pattern.

Make the first two shots simpler.

Walls & defence · Updated 18 August 2026

Against pace, the back glass can give you the time your hands do not have.

Fast drives feel intimidating when you try to intercept everything before the wall. Letting a deep ball rebound can turn an urgent reaction into a slower, more readable second trajectory.

First decide whether the ball will rebound usefully

If a hard ball lands deep and travels into the back wall with energy, the rebound is likely to come back into court. Give it space. If the ball lands short and arrives at the glass already dying, take it earlier if you can do so comfortably.

Do not stand on top of the glass

When you are too close, the rebound arrives into your body and appears much faster than it really is. Create enough space that the ball can leave the wall before you make contact.

Use a compact swing

The incoming pace is already doing work for you. A huge backswing reduces timing margin. Prepare early, keep the racket movement economical and prioritise clean contact.

Watch the rebound angle

Topspin, slice and the position of the bounce can change how the ball leaves the glass. You are not hitting the trajectory that entered the wall; you are hitting the new trajectory that leaves it.

That sounds obvious until you notice how many players move towards the original line and then reach sideways for the rebound.

Your best reply may be a reset

After defending a heavy ball, you do not need to produce an immediate winner. A controlled lob can buy time and challenge the opponents' net position. A low ball through the middle can reduce their angles. The success is getting out of pressure with shape intact.

When to counterattack

If the rebound sits high and you are balanced, the glass may actually create an attacking contact. That is the basis of shots such as the bajada. But do not decide to attack before you have seen the height of the rebound.

A simple practice habit

During warm-up, deliberately let several suitable balls reach the back glass instead of intercepting them. Focus on spacing and where the rebound peaks. Comfort with the wall comes from seeing the same geometry repeatedly.

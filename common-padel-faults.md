# The common padel faults that actually cost you points.

Most rules arguments are not about obscure tournament regulations. They are about the same handful of situations: the net, two bounces, the fence, a body touch or two partners trying to hit the same ball.

Reviewed against Rule 13 and related sections of the current International Padel Federation Rules of Padel.

## Touching the net while the ball is live

If you, your racket or anything you are wearing or carrying touches the net, net posts, tension cable or the opponent's court while the ball is in play, you lose the point.

That includes the obvious case of falling into the net after a volley, but it also includes a racket brushing it during follow-through. Wait until the point is over before leaning on or crossing into anything around the net.

## Letting the ball bounce twice

The ball can bounce once on your side and then use the glass or fence, but you must return it before it bounces on the ground a second time. A second ground bounce ends the point.

This is why learning to use the glass matters. The wall itself is not the extra bounce. The floor is.

## Hitting the ball before it crosses the net

You cannot reach into the opponent's side and strike the ball before it has crossed the plane of the net. There are legal situations where a ball bounces in your opponents' court and then comes back over the net, but the sequence matters. You cannot simply intercept an incoming ball early on their side.

## Sending the ball straight into the opponent's wall or fence

A normal return has to reach the opponent's court correctly. If you hit the ball directly into their glass or metallic fence without it bouncing on the ground first, you lose the point.

You may use your own glass to return the ball, provided the ball then travels over the net and bounces correctly in the opponents' court. The distinction is easy to remember: ** your own glass can be part of the shot; their wall cannot be the first landing. **

## Double hits

Deliberately or clearly hitting the ball twice loses the point. The current rules do allow a contact that occurs in one natural movement where the ball is not deliberately played twice and its natural exit is not substantially changed, but two separate swings are not legal.

If you mishit a volley and feel two distinct contacts, do not assume it is automatically legal because they happened quickly.

## Both partners hitting the ball

Only one player from the pair may play the ball. If both partners hit it simultaneously or one after the other, the point is lost.

There is a useful exception in the rules: if both players try to take the ball, one actually hits it and the other only hits their partner's racket, that is not treated as both players hitting the ball.

## The ball hits you or your partner

If the opponents' shot hits your body, clothing or equipment instead of your racket as part of a legal return, you lose the point. You also lose the point if your own return then touches you, your partner or something either of you is wearing.

That is why moving together matters. Crowding the same ball creates both tactical problems and very literal ways to lose the rally.

## Hitting your own fence or floor

You can deliberately use your own glass as part of a legal return. The metallic fence is different. If your shot touches the metallic fence or ground on your own side before crossing the net, the point is lost.

The glass is a playing surface you can use tactically. The fence is not a substitute backboard for your own shot.

## Two service faults

A first-serve fault gives you another attempt. A second consecutive fault loses the point. Common service faults include missing the correct diagonal box, striking above the permitted height, foot faults and a legal first bounce that then reaches the metallic fence before the second bounce.

For the full setup, see [ padel service rules ](https://padelwrist.com/padel-service-rules/) and [ padel let rules ](https://padelwrist.com/padel-let-rules/) .

## Dropping the racket or breaking the safety cord

Under the current FIP rules, if a player drops their racket or the safety cord breaks during the point, the pair immediately loses that point. The wrist strap is not just a recommendation: it is part of safe, legal play.

## A practical way to learn the rules

Do not try to memorise every clause at once. Start with five anchors: one bounce, do not touch the net, the opponents' court must receive the ball before their wall, only one partner hits it, and keep the racket attached.

Those rules cover a large share of what new players actually encounter. Then add service, tie-break and out-of-court details as your matches become more competitive.

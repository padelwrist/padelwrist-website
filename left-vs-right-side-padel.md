# Left side or right side?

There is no rule saying one personality belongs on each side. The useful question is how your two strengths fit together and who is most comfortable taking which balls through the middle.

## The traditional shorthand

For two right-handed players, the left-side player has a forehand toward the middle and is often expected to cover more overheads and take attacking responsibility. The right-side player is often described as the steadier builder. Those are common tactical roles, not laws.

## Why the middle matters

Many balls arrive between partners. A right-handed player on the left can attack a central overhead or ground ball with the forehand, which is one reason strong attacking players often favour that side.

## What about a left-handed player?

A left-hander on the right side can also bring a forehand and overhead into the middle. A right-handed/left-handed pair can therefore have attacking forehands covering the centre from both sides, but communication is essential because both may naturally want the same ball.

## Right-side responsibilities

The right-side player commonly handles a high volume of controlled returns, defensive balls and construction. Consistency, a reliable lob and good decision-making are valuable. Calling it the “defensive side” can be misleading: both players need to attack and defend.

## Left-side responsibilities

The left-side player commonly takes more responsibility for central overheads and finishing opportunities. That does not mean smashing everything. Choosing the right bandeja, víbora or lob response matters more than simply being the hardest hitter.

## How should beginners choose?

Try both. Pick the side where returns feel comfortable and your partnership has the clearest middle-ball rule. Beginners gain more from stable spacing and communication than from forcing themselves into stereotyped roles.

Give each side more than one game before deciding. The return of serve, defending the corner and taking central lobs can feel very different from left to right. A short practice set on each side usually tells you more than choosing based on whether you think of yourself as an attacking or defensive player.

## Do you stay on the same side all match?

Pairs commonly establish preferred sides, but movement during rallies is fluid and serving formations can alter starting positions. The important thing is recovering a sensible pair shape rather than sprinting back to a painted imaginary lane after every shot.

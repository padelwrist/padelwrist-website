Walls & defence · Updated 27 August 2026

The corner stops looking chaotic once you read which wall comes first.

Balls entering the back corner can rebound from one wall or two. Your job is to identify the likely sequence early, create space and move towards the exit path rather than following the ball into the corner.

Start with the first wall

A ball that reaches the back glass first will usually leave it towards the side before the second contact. A ball that reaches the side glass first can open back towards the middle after the next rebound. The exact angle depends on pace and spin, but identifying the first surface gives you a much better starting prediction.

Do not chase the ball into the corner

Beginners often move towards the point of impact and end up trapped behind the rebound. Instead, move towards the space where the ball is likely to emerge after the wall contact.

Give double-glass balls time to finish the sequence

If the ball has enough depth and angle to use both walls, reaching in between contacts can make the shot harder. Let the rebound develop when you have time and position to do so.

Use small adjustment steps

Large early guesses are risky because spin can change the final exit. Get into the correct general area, then use smaller steps while watching the second rebound.

Keep the reply simple under pressure

After a difficult corner defence, a safe lob or controlled ball through the middle may be more valuable than trying to attack from a stretched position. Escaping the corner with balance is already a tactical success.

When can you attack from the corner?

If the rebound comes out high and you are balanced, you may have time to drive or play a bajada. Let the height of the ball decide. Do not pre-select an attacking shot while you are still reading two walls.

A simple practice pattern

Have a partner feed alternating balls that reach back glass first and side glass first. Do not try to win the rally. Call the first wall out loud, move to the predicted exit and play a controlled lob. That trains the read and footwork separately from shot selection, which is much easier than trying to learn everything in live points.

Learn the rebound family

Continue with double-glass rebounds, when to use the glass and fast back-glass defence.

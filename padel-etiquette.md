# Padel etiquette.

Most of it is simple: be ready, call things clearly, respect the booking after yours, and remember that your partner is not a customer-service department for your missed volleys.

## Arrive before the booking starts

A 60- or 90-minute court slot disappears quickly if four people are still changing shoes at the start time. Arrive early enough to check in, collect hire equipment and be ready when the court becomes available.

## Agree the format before the first serve

Advantage, deciding point, one set, best of three, timed finish or Fixed Points: decide it first. Most avoidable scoring arguments begin with four people assuming they are playing the same format.

## Call the score

The server should make the score clear before serving, particularly after a long rally or disputed point. If somebody is unsure, resolve it before the next point begins.

## Stray balls

If a ball from another court creates a safety risk, stop and return it safely rather than playing through it. Likewise, do not walk onto another active court to collect a ball without getting the players' attention.

## Line and bounce calls

In social play, give opponents the benefit of genuine uncertainty. Endless arguments over a marginal bounce make a friendly match much worse than one incorrectly awarded point.

## Talk to your partner

Call lobs, middle balls and switches. Useful communication is short and early. Critiquing your partner's technique after every error is neither.

A good rule is to communicate information your partner cannot easily see, rather than narrating every rally. Early calls such as “yours”, “mine”, “switch” or a clear warning about an opponent moving forward are useful. A frustrated post-point lecture usually is not.

## Warm-up is not the final

Use the warm-up to find timing, test the glass and give everyone a reasonable number of balls. Smashing every feed at full pace rarely helps anybody prepare.

## Respect different levels

Social padel often mixes abilities. If the session is not explicitly competitive, avoid targeting the least experienced player on every rally simply because it is the easiest route to a point. A good match gives everyone a chance to play, improve and leave wanting another game.

## Finish on time

If another booking follows yours, leave the court promptly. If your match is unfinished, agree the result or stopping point rather than assuming the next group should wait.

Collect balls and belongings before the booking expires where possible. The next group should not lose the first five minutes of their session because the previous match decided to squeeze in “one last game”.

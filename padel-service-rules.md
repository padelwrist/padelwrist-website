# Padel service rules.

The serve is underarm, starts with a bounce and travels diagonally into the opposite service box. Once those three ideas are clear, the rest is mostly positioning, order and knowing what counts as a fault.

Reviewed against the current International Padel Federation Rules of Padel.

## Where does the server stand?

The server starts behind the service line, between the centre line's imaginary continuation and the side wall. At least one foot must remain in contact with the ground when the ball is struck. The server must not step on the service line before contact.

## Bounce first, then strike below waist height

The server bounces the ball in the serving area and strikes it at or below waist height. The ball travels diagonally across the net into the receiver's service box. The opening point of a game is served from the right, then the serving side alternates after each point.

## What makes a serve a fault?

A serve is a fault if it misses the correct service box, is struck illegally, or lands correctly and then hits the metal fence. A legal first bounce followed by the glass can remain in play.

There are several other ways to lose a point once the rally starts. See [ common padel faults ](https://padelwrist.com/common-padel-faults/) for the practical list.

## Do you get a second serve?

Yes. A fault on the first serve gives the server a second attempt. Two consecutive service faults lose the point.

## What is a let?

If the serve touches the net and then otherwise lands legally, it can be replayed as a let under the relevant rule. A net touch does not automatically make every serve a let: the subsequent landing still matters.

The exact first-serve, second-serve and fence scenarios are covered in [ padel let rules ](https://padelwrist.com/padel-let-rules/) .

## Serving order in doubles

Before a set, each pair decides which player serves first. The two partners then alternate service games for that set. The order can be chosen again at the start of the next set.

## What changes in a tie-break?

The player whose turn it is serves the first point. Service then moves through the established order in two-point blocks. See [ padel tie-break rules ](https://padelwrist.com/padel-tie-break-rules/) for the full sequence.

## Rules tell you what is legal. Technique makes it useful.

Once the legal action is comfortable, the next step is not simply serving harder. A deeper, more accurate serve can give the receiver less room and make your first volley easier. See [ how to improve your padel serve ](https://padelwrist.com/improve-padel-serve-control/) for depth, placement, slice and consistency.

The serve also starts a positional pattern. After contact, move forwards and prepare for the return rather than standing behind the service line to watch the ball. Our guide to [ what to do after serving ](https://padelwrist.com/what-to-do-after-serving-padel/) covers that transition.

## A useful beginner routine

Before serving, check three things: the score, which side you should serve from and whether you have the correct diagonal box. That two-second routine prevents most casual-match mistakes. If the first serve is a fault, reset properly rather than rushing the second attempt because you are frustrated.

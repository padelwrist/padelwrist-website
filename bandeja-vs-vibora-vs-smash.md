# Three overheads. Three different jobs.

When a lob comes over your head, the question is not “which overhead looks best?” It is whether you need to preserve the net, increase pressure or try to finish the point.

## Bandeja: control and keep the net

The bandeja is the default answer to many good lobs. It is a controlled, sliced overhead designed to send the ball deep while allowing you to recover forward and maintain net position.

Choose it when the lob pushes you backwards, your balance is not ideal, or a full smash would be a gamble. The goal is usually not an immediate winner.

[Learn the bandeja technique →](https://padelwrist.com/how-to-hit-bandeja-padel/)

## Víbora: more aggressive pressure

The víbora is also an overhead with slice, but it is generally played with more speed and sidespin and with greater attacking intent. You are trying to create a difficult, low rebound while still retaining control of the net.

It tends to suit a ball that gives you better position than a defensive bandeja but does not necessarily justify a full finishing smash.

[Learn how to hit a víbora →](https://padelwrist.com/how-to-hit-vibora-padel/)

## Smash: attack a genuine opportunity

A smash makes sense when the lob is short enough, you are balanced and you can contact the ball from a strong attacking position. Depending on level and technique, the goal might be to bring the ball back over the net, send it out of court or simply create a rebound the opponents cannot defend.

Trying the same shot while moving backwards turns a high-value attacking option into a low-percentage rescue attempt.

## A simple decision table

**Deep lob, moving backwards:** usually bandeja or retreat.

**Comfortable overhead with room to accelerate and slice:** víbora can add pressure.

**Short lob, balanced, strong contact:** consider the smash.

**Ball already behind you:** stop forcing an overhead and defend the next phase.

## Why beginners overuse the smash

A high ball looks attackable, but height alone does not make it a good smash. Contact point, balance and court position matter. A smash from behind the service line can rebound off the back glass and give the opponents a comfortable counterattack.

## The overhead is part of your positioning

Good shot choice is judged by what happens next. If your bandeja lets you recover to the net, it has done its job even if the opponents return it. If an impressive smash forces you to retreat while they counter-lob, the rally may actually have become worse for you.

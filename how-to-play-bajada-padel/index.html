Shots & technique · Updated 29 September 2026

The bajada starts with space, not with power.

A bajada is an attacking ball played after a high rebound from the back glass. The opportunity comes from the rebound lifting the ball into a comfortable contact zone. If you chase the glass or hit while still moving backwards, that opportunity disappears.

What is a bajada?

“Bajada” means a ball coming down. In padel, it usually describes an aggressive shot played after a lob or deep ball bounces and rises from the back glass. Instead of simply defending the rebound, you use the height to play forwards with pace, slice or placement.

It is not available on every glass rebound. If the ball stays low or remains behind you, a controlled defensive return or lob is the better choice.

Move back early, then stop chasing the ball

The most important movement happens before the glass. As soon as you recognise that the ball will travel deep, move back early enough to create space between yourself and the wall.

Do not run directly to the point where the ball will hit the glass. The rebound is coming back into the court. Position for where the ball will be after the rebound, not where it touches the wall.

Let the rebound come into your hitting zone

A useful bajada gives you the ball in front of your body and at a height from which you can drive forwards. If you are cramped against the glass, you lose both the swing and the ability to move through contact.

It is normally easier to start with slightly too much space and adjust forwards than to stand too close and discover there is nowhere for the ball or racket to go.

Prepare after you know the rebound

Prepare early enough that you are not rushed, but avoid fixing the racket at one height before you have read the rebound. The ball can leave the glass higher or lower depending on speed, spin and angle.

Once the path is clear, set the racket compactly and organise your feet so you can strike while balanced and moving forwards rather than rotating on your heels.

Attack with control

The bajada can be hit with slice and good pace, but it still needs margin. A huge backswing makes direction harder and advertises the shot to your opponents. Compact preparation lets you use the height of the rebound without turning every bajada into an all-or-nothing winner attempt.

Common targets include the middle, the feet of the net players and spaces that make the first volley difficult. The goal is often to create a weak reply that lets you advance.

Use the shot to move forward

A strong bajada should change your court position as well as the ball. If your shot puts the opponents under pressure, move forwards with your partner and look to recover the net. Staying at the back after a successful attacking rebound gives the opponents time to reset.

The transition only works if the quality of the shot justifies it. If your bajada floats short or high, stay patient rather than running into an easy volley.

Forehand or backhand?

You do not always have time to run around the ball for a forehand. A central or awkward rebound may be better attacked with the backhand, especially if moving around it would leave you late or out of position.

The principle is the same on both sides: create space, let the ball travel in front and use a compact forward action.

Common bajada mistakes

Running into the glass: you get jammed by the rebound.

Hitting while still retreating: balance and direction disappear.

Preparing too big: power increases while accuracy drops.

Attacking a low rebound: not every ball off the glass is a bajada opportunity.

Watching the shot instead of recovering: you miss the chance to turn pressure into net position.

Learn the glass before attacking from it.

Start with when to use the glass and defending fast balls off the back glass, then add the bajada when the rebound gives you height and time.

# Do not try to beat a tennis player by playing tennis inside a padel court.

A good tennis player can arrive at padel with excellent timing, pace and hand skills. Your advantage is that padel rewards different geometry: walls, two-player movement, controlled net pressure and shot selection matter as much as raw ball speed.

## Why tennis players can feel overwhelming

Players with a tennis background often strike a cleaner, faster ball than a new padel player expects. If you stand too far forwards at the back or try to volley every drive, their pace can rush you into errors.

The wrong response is to hit even harder. That turns the rally into the type of exchange they are most comfortable playing.

## Let the court absorb some of the pace

Deep, hard balls are often easier after the back glass than before it. Give yourself room, stay calm and use the rebound to reduce the feeling that every drive needs an instant reaction.

The glass changes the value of power. A shot that would be a clean winner on a tennis court may rebound into a playable ball in padel.

## Make them hit overheads, not groundstrokes

If the opponents are comfortable trading drives from the back, a good lob changes the question. It forces them to move backwards, judge an overhead and decide whether to bandeja, víbora, smash or retreat.

You are trying to move the rally away from their strongest pattern and towards specifically padel decisions.

## Take the net when you earn it

Do not assume a hard hitter staying at the baseline means you should stay there too. If your lob pushes them back, move forward together. At the net, use compact volleys, the middle and low placement instead of feeding comfortable rebounds.

## Do not give them easy pace off your own glass

Overhitting volleys into the back wall can produce a lively rebound at exactly the height a tennis player enjoys. Depth is useful, but so are angle, low trajectory and playing into feet or the middle.

## Use patience as a tactic

A tennis convert may be physically capable of hitting winners from positions where most padel players would not try. That does not mean every hard shot is high percentage. Make them play one more overhead, one more wall ball and one more awkward volley.

You do not need to prove you can match their best stroke. You need to keep putting the ball into situations where padel knowledge matters.

## What if they refuse to come to the net?

Do not automatically copy them. Keep the net when you can do so safely and avoid giving away easy passing angles. If they are comfortable defending deep, vary the height and pace of your volleys and look for balls to the middle or feet rather than hitting every shot towards the back glass.

## For tennis players reading this

Your existing skills are an advantage. The quickest adaptation is usually not learning a completely new forehand; it is changing what you consider a good court position and learning when the glass should become part of the rally.

Shorter swings, more lobs, more patient overheads and moving with your partner will make your tennis skills much more dangerous in a padel context.

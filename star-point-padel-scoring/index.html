Star Point: the 2026 padel scoring change explained.

Star Point changes what happens when a game reaches deuce. It keeps some traditional advantage play, but stops a game from cycling through 40-40 indefinitely.

Reviewed against the current International Padel Federation Rules of Padel and 2026 Star Point guidance.

The short version

At 40-40, Star Point does not immediately jump to one deciding rally. Instead, the game allows two advantage opportunities. If neither side converts those opportunities, the next deciding Star Point settles the game.

First deuce: play an advantage point

If advantage is lost: return to deuce

Second deuce: play another advantage point

If that advantage is lost: move to Star Point

Star Point: one rally decides the game

How the sequence works

Imagine the score reaches 40-40. Team One wins the next rally and takes advantage. If Team One wins again, the game is over. If Team Two wins that advantage point, the score returns to deuce.

From that second deuce, the same thing happens once more. The next rally creates advantage. If the advantaged team converts it, the game ends. If the advantage is lost again, the game moves to the Star Point.

The Star Point is then a single deciding rally. Whoever wins it wins the game. That creates a hard limit on how long deuce can continue.

Who receives the Star Point serve?

Under the FIP format, the receiving pair chooses whether the deciding serve is received on the right or left side. The receiving pair cannot swap positions purely for that point. In mixed competition, the relevant competition rules may also specify who receives based on the server's sex.

This is one of the reasons it is useful to know the format before the match begins. Star Point changes more than the label on the scoreboard: it also changes the pressure and receiving decision at the end of a close game.

Star Point vs Golden Point

Golden Point is more abrupt. In a deciding-point format, deuce is followed by one rally that wins the game. Star Point preserves some of the traditional advantage structure first.

That makes Star Point a middle ground. Traditional advantage can continue through repeated deuce cycles with no fixed limit. Golden Point ends the game immediately after deuce. Star Point allows two advantage chances and then forces a decision.

For a full comparison, read Golden Point vs Advantage vs Star Point.

Where is Star Point used?

The International Padel Federation introduced the format for 2026 across specified circuits including Premier Padel, the CUPRA FIP Tour, FIP Promises and FIP Beyond. That does not mean every padel match everywhere automatically uses Star Point.

Club leagues, local competitions and social matches may still use traditional advantage, Golden Point or another agreed scoring system. The organiser's rules take priority. If you are playing casually, agree the deuce format before the first serve rather than discovering the difference at 40-40.

Why was it introduced?

FIP described the change as an attempt to balance tradition with predictability. Long advantage games can be dramatic, but they can also make match duration difficult to control. Star Point keeps the familiar tension of advantage while guaranteeing that a game cannot continue through endless deuce cycles.

For players, the practical effect is simple: the first two deuce cycles still reward converting an advantage, but there is a clear final rally waiting if neither side can do it.

What should you remember on court?

The easiest mistake is treating Star Point as another name for Golden Point. It is not. Remember the sequence: advantage, advantage, deciding point.

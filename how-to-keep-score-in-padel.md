# How to keep score in padel without thinking about it all match.

The rules are only half the problem. The other half is remembering the score after a long rally, who is serving, and whether you are changing ends. A simple routine makes that much easier.

## Remember the three layers

Think of a normal padel match in three layers: points build a game, games build a set, and sets decide the match. Point scores usually run love, 15, 30 and 40. Six games normally wins a set if the pair is two games clear. A standard match is usually best of three sets.

## Call the score before every serve

The easiest habit is also the oldest: say the score out loud before serving. Call the serving side's score first. Doing this every time gives all four players a chance to correct a mistake before the next rally starts.

If nobody can remember whether it is 30-15 or 30-30, stopping for ten seconds is better than carrying uncertainty through another three points.

## Track the server as carefully as the points

Each pair chooses a serving order for the set and keeps that order until the set ends. A score can be correct while the wrong player serves, so it helps to treat the server as part of the match state rather than an afterthought.

If you use a score tracker, a visible server indicator is often more useful than an elaborate statistics screen during play.

## Know the moments where the system changes

At 40-40, the next step depends on whether you are playing advantage, golden point or another agreed format. At 6-6 in a standard set, you normally move into a tie-break. In the tie-break, count 0, 1, 2, 3 rather than 15, 30, 40.

Agree those choices before the match. Most score disputes are easier to prevent than resolve.

## Change ends without losing the thread

Players change ends after the first, third and every subsequent odd game in the set. During a tie-break, change ends after every six points. If you are using an app or watch, side-change reminders can remove one more thing to remember.

## Paper, phone or watch?

For a casual match, calling the score may be enough. A phone works well if one person off court is scoring. When the players themselves are keeping score, a watch is usually quicker because it stays attached and can be updated immediately after the rally.

PadelWrist Events.

From a casual club Americano to an organised Mexicano evening, PadelWrist Events aims to make the entire session easier to run. It is a separate feature under active development, not a tournament manager you can use in the released app today.

Run the event. Stay close to the court.

PadelWrist already handles individual match scoring. Events takes on a different job: helping the person organising an entire session. We are designing an organiser-first experience inside the existing PadelWrist iPhone and iPad app, rather than asking a coach or club host to learn a separate desktop system.

The proposed experience covers creating an event, preparing a draw, assigning courts, recording official results and showing a clear individual leaderboard. The organiser will decide when a round is ready, when the next one starts and when the event ends. These workflows are under active product evaluation and are not public production features yet.

Americano and Mexicano, properly distinguished

Americano typically uses planned partner rotations across short, fixed-point matches. Points contribute to individual totals even as partnerships change. The draw should make social mixing and fairness easy to understand.

Mexicano also builds individual points totals, but later rounds use the latest confirmed standings to propose new pairings. The organiser needs to review those pairings before publishing them, not have active fixtures silently rearranged after a result is corrected.

There is no universal set of social-tournament rules. Clubs vary their point targets, rest policies, tie-breakers and how often they rotate. The Events concept aims to explain the chosen rules, not hide them.

What we are exploring

  • Native event creation on iPhone and iPad, with a named player roster and available courts
  • Americano and Mexicano event formats, with a proposed 16 combined-rally-point starting default that organisers can review
  • Flexible, organiser-driven rounds rather than an enforced number of rounds
  • Draw previews, court assignments, visible off-court players and organiser-controlled publication
  • Official scores recorded and confirmed by an organiser, with transparent corrections
  • Individual standings and a clear distinction between provisional and final results
  • An optional read-only way for participants to follow fixtures, scores and rankings without editing them

These are product goals and discovery work. The precise behaviour for withdrawals, substitutes, unequal playing time, repeated partners and corrected results is still being validated. Screens or prototype examples should not be mistaken for a released service.

A typical club evening

Imagine eight players booking two courts for a social session. The organiser chooses Americano or Mexicano, enters the players, sets a points target and reviews the proposed matches. Each court hosts a four-player match; the organiser confirms the resulting scores. Players can then see where they stand and who they are due to play with in the next round.

In Americano, those future rotations can be planned to distribute partnerships. In Mexicano, the next draw is based on the results confirmed so far. A club can choose another round if time allows rather than committing to a fixed-length competition at the start. This is an illustrative goal for the product, not a description of currently available app screens.

What Events is not

PadelWrist Events is not a court booking platform, registration or fee-collection service, and it is not a replacement for the free match scorekeeper. The initial product direction is intentionally focused on the organiser's draw, courts, results and standings. We have not announced an Events launch date, public pricing, paid tiers or a registration programme.

Apple Watch tournament administration is not part of the proposed initial scope. The Watch remains focused on keeping score during an individual match.

Help shape the experience

Do you run weekly Americanos, Mexicano nights or small club competitions? We are interested in how you handle late arrivals, byes, player changes, disputed scores and results at the end of a session. Real organiser feedback matters more than a polished demonstration using ideal eight-player groups.

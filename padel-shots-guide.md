# The padel shots people keep naming mid-rally.

You do not need a Spanish vocabulary test to play padel. But understanding what a bandeja, víbora or chiquita is helps you understand what problem each shot is trying to solve.

## Start with the boring shots

Before the specialist overheads, get comfortable with a controlled forehand, backhand, volley and lob. Padel rewards repeatable placement more than occasional spectacular winners.

## Lob

A high ball over the opponents' heads, usually used to push them away from the net. The lob is one of the most important shots in padel because it changes court position. A good lob is deep enough to force retreat without giving away an easy overhead.

## Volley

A shot taken before the ball bounces. At the net, the aim is often to keep the ball low, move opponents and create the next easier ball rather than finish the point immediately.

## Bandeja

A controlled sliced overhead, usually hit when a lob is too deep for a normal attacking smash but not deep enough to force you fully back. Its main job is to preserve net position and keep the opponents under pressure.

## Víbora

A more aggressive sliced overhead than the bandeja, often hit with greater side-spin and pace. The intention is to make the rebound awkward and keep the ball low after the glass.

## Chiquita

A soft, low shot aimed around the opponents' feet while they are at the net. It can force them to volley upward and create a chance for you to move forward. It is about precision and timing, not speed.

## Bajada

An attacking ball played after a high rebound from your back glass. Rather than merely defending the wall, you use the higher contact to drive the ball forward and put the net pair under pressure.

## Smash

A hard overhead aimed at ending the point or creating an unreturnable rebound. Beginners often overuse it. If the ball is not in the right position, a controlled bandeja is usually the smarter option.

## What should beginners learn first?

In order: a reliable return, lob, basic volley, glass defence and controlled overhead. The more specialised attacking shots become useful once you can consistently keep the rally alive and understand where your partner is.

The useful question is not “what is the most advanced shot I can learn?” but “what shot solves the ball I am receiving?” A simple lob or controlled volley chosen at the right time is usually more valuable than forcing a víbora from the wrong position.

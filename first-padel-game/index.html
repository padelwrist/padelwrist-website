Getting started

Your first padel game.

You do not need lessons, expensive kit or a full understanding of the rulebook before you walk onto court. You need four players, shoes you can move sideways in, and five minutes of useful context.

Before you arrive

Check whether the venue includes racket hire and balls. Wear ordinary sports clothing and court shoes if you have them. Bring water. If you have never played, tell the other players rather than spending twenty minutes pretending the glass makes perfect sense.

The five things worth knowing

Serve underarm. Bounce the ball and strike it below waist height into the diagonal service box.

The serve must bounce on return. You cannot volley the return of serve.

One floor bounce. During the rally, the ball can bounce once on your side before you hit it.

The glass can help you. After that first bounce, the ball may rebound from your wall and still be playable.

Score like tennis. Love, 15, 30, 40 and game, with the deuce format agreed before play.

Do not try to hit everything hard

The easiest beginner mistake is assuming harder is better. A controlled ball that stays low creates more problems than a wild smash that rebounds comfortably from the glass. Get the ball back and learn what the court does.

Let the glass work

When a ball passes you toward the back wall, resist the instinct to chase it into the corner. Give it room to bounce and rebound. The first few times feel wrong; then the geometry suddenly starts to make sense.

Move with your partner

If one of you goes to the net and the other stays deep, opponents get a large diagonal gap to target. Try to defend together and attack together. You do not need perfect positioning, just a shared idea of where the pair is moving.

Scorekeeping

Call the score clearly and confirm the server if there is any uncertainty. The best time to resolve a score disagreement is immediately, not three rallies later when everybody remembers a different version.

What success looks like

Not winning. If you finish the session understanding the serve, returning a few balls after the glass and beginning to move with your partner, you have learned the parts that make the next game dramatically easier.

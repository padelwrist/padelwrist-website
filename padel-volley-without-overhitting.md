# A padel volley does not need to be hard to be aggressive.

At the net, your advantage comes from taking the ball early and controlling where the opponents have to play next. Hitting harder than necessary can create a lively back-glass rebound and give away the pressure you worked to build.

## Keep the preparation short

The ball reaches you quickly at the net. A compact preparation gives you more time to adjust to direction and keeps the racket stable. Think block and guide before you think full swing.

## Use the opponents' pace

On a fast incoming ball, you often do not need to create much extra speed. A firm racket face and clean contact can redirect the ball with enough pace while preserving control.

## Make the contact height work for you

A volley above net height gives you more attacking options. A low volley around your feet is a very different shot: lift enough to clear the net, keep the target safe and recover. Trying to hit a winner from below net height is a common way to lose an otherwise neutral point.

## Useful targets beat random power

Through the middle reduces the opponents' angles and can create hesitation. Towards the feet forces a lower contact. Deep corners can pin a defender behind the ball. The right target depends on where both opponents are, not on which direction feels most spectacular.

## Why a huge volley can be counterproductive

Hard balls that reach the back glass can rebound quickly and comfortably into a defender's hitting zone. If your opponent is already deep and balanced, another ten percent of pace may help them more than you.

## Move after the volley

Do not freeze to watch your shot. Shift with your partner towards the likely reply and keep the pair connected. Good volleying is part racket skill and part court shape.

## The progression to practise

First learn to volley ten controlled balls to a large target. Then add direction. Only then add pace. If speed causes your contact, balance or recovery to deteriorate, it is not yet useful speed.

A simple test is whether you are ready for the next ball. If a harder volley leaves you leaning forward, admiring the shot or scrambling to recover, reduce the pace until you can finish balanced and move immediately with your partner.

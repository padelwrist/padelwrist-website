Playing better

Padel positioning for beginners.

The most useful positioning rule in padel is not a precise distance from a line. It is simpler: move as a pair. When one player attacks and the other stays behind, the court opens up.

Think in two positions

For a beginner, the court is easiest to understand as two broad states. You are either defending from the back or attacking from the net. The messy middle is where many easy points are lost.

When serving

After serving, move forward to join your partner at the net. The serve is not usually the attacking weapon it is in tennis; its job is to begin the point and help the serving pair establish net position.

When returning

The receiver must let the serve bounce. Start deep enough to deal with a ball that reaches the glass. A controlled return through the middle or toward the server's feet is often more useful than trying to hit a clean winner.

At the net

Do not stand on top of the net. Give yourself enough room to react to a lob while still being close enough to volley comfortably. Move left and right with your partner so the pair protects the same half of the court together.

If your opponents send a good lob over you, both players should generally retreat rather than leaving one player stranded at the net.

Defending the back glass

When defending, give the ball enough room to reach the glass. Beginners often stand too close to the back wall and then have nowhere to move when the ball rebounds. Leave a workable gap and learn to turn with the trajectory rather than swinging at every ball before the glass.

The middle ball

“Yours or mine?” loses points. Agree a simple rule with your partner for balls through the middle. Forehand priority is common, but communication matters more than pretending there is one universal answer for every pair.

Why the lob matters

A good lob pushes the attacking pair away from the net and creates time for you and your partner to move forward. It is not merely a defensive escape shot; it is one of the main ways possession of the net changes hands.

The no-man's-land problem

Standing halfway between the baseline and net for too long gives you the worst of both worlds. Fast volleys arrive at your feet, while lobs still pass over you. Transition through that area with a purpose rather than treating it as a home position.

A practical rule for your next match

Before every point, ask one question: are we defending or attacking? If the answer is different for each partner, fix the spacing before worrying about the next shot.

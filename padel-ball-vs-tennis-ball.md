# Padel ball vs tennis ball.

They look almost identical in a photo. They are not quite the same in play, and the difference matters most when the ball starts rebounding from the glass.

## The short answer

Padel balls and tennis balls are both pressurised felt-covered rubber balls, but padel has its own official specification. Padel balls are generally specified at a lower internal pressure than standard tennis balls, affecting speed and rebound.

## Why pressure matters

More pressure generally means a livelier rebound. In an enclosed court where the back glass is part of the rally, small changes in bounce alter how much time a defender has and how high the ball returns from the wall.

You notice this most when defending. A livelier ball comes off the glass faster and can push you further back, while a softer ball may die sooner and force you to move forward. The difference is subtle compared with changing sports entirely, but after a few matches it becomes easy to feel.

## Can you use tennis balls?

For informal practice, players sometimes do. For a match you want to feel like padel, use balls made and approved for padel. Tournament play should follow the ball requirements of the governing body or event.

If a club hands you a can of padel balls, use them rather than substituting a random set from a tennis bag. Consistent equipment makes it easier to judge lobs, wall rebounds and overheads, especially when you are still learning what a normal padel bounce looks like.

## Why old balls feel different

Pressurised balls lose pressure after opening. As they age, they become softer and less lively. That changes serves, smashes and glass rebounds, which is why a fresh can can make the same court feel noticeably faster.

## Do altitude and temperature matter?

Yes. Air conditions affect ball behaviour, which is one reason governing bodies approve different ball types and competition organisers specify equipment. For normal club play, the practical answer is simply to use appropriate padel balls in decent condition.

## What should beginners buy?

Do not overthink brands at the start. Buy a recognised padel ball from a reputable sports retailer or use what your club supplies. Learning to read the glass and control depth will make a far bigger difference than switching between similar approved balls.

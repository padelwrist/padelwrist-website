Scoring formats

Padel does not have just one way to finish a close game.

The familiar 15, 30 and 40 remains the backbone of standard padel, but modern rules allow several ways to handle deuce and deciding sets. Social formats can look different again.

Advantage scoring

At 40-40, play moves to deuce. One point gives advantage; a second consecutive point wins the game. If the pair with advantage loses the next rally, the score returns to deuce. The process can repeat without a fixed limit.

Golden Point

Golden Point removes advantage. At 40-40, one deciding rally is played and the receiving pair chooses the receiving side. The winner of that rally wins the game. It is popular when a competition or club wants more predictable match lengths.

Star Point

Star Point was added to the FIP Rules of Padel for 2026. It keeps a limited amount of advantage play, but if the game reaches a third deuce, a deciding Star Point is played. It sits between unlimited advantage and the immediate sudden-death of golden point.

Standard tie-break

At 6-6 in a normal set, a tie-break is typically played to seven points with a two-point margin. The winner takes the set 7-6. The serving sequence changes to one opening serve, then two serves per player in the established order.

Match tie-break and super tie-break

When agreed in advance, a deciding tie-break can replace the third set. FIP's alternative methods include a seven-point match tie-break and a ten-point super tie-break, both requiring a two-point margin.

Mini set

An alternative short-set format uses four games rather than six, with a two-game margin. Under the FIP alternative method, a tie-break is played if the score reaches 4-4.

Fixed Points

Social sessions such as Americano and Mexicano often abandon 15-30-40 entirely for the round itself. Each rally counts as one point and the round ends at an agreed total or target. These are organiser-defined social formats rather than one universal FIP match format.

Agree the format before you start

Two pairs can both say they are playing “standard padel” and still have different expectations at deuce or in the deciding set. A 20-second conversation before the first serve prevents a much less enjoyable argument at 40-40.

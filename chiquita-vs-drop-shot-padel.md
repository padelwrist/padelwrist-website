# A chiquita is not just a padel word for a drop shot.

Both shots use touch and reduced pace, which is why they get confused. The important difference is where the opponents are and what you are trying to make them do next.

## What is a chiquita?

A chiquita is usually played from the back or transition area towards opponents who control the net. The aim is to make the ball pass low over the net and drop around their feet, forcing them to volley upwards or contact below net height.

The shot is often part of a transition. If the opponents are forced to play a weak upward ball, you may be able to move forward and challenge for the net.

## What is a drop shot?

A drop shot is an attacking short ball designed to die near the net when the opponents are positioned deep enough that reaching it is difficult. You are trying to exploit empty space in front of them.

It is usually a much worse idea when the opponents are already standing at the net. In that situation you would simply be playing a soft ball towards the people closest to it.

## The easiest way to remember the difference

**Chiquita:** opponents up, you are trying to put the ball at their feet and create a weak volley.

**Drop shot:** opponents back, you are trying to make the ball stop in the empty court in front of them.

## Why the chiquita is difficult

Too high and the opponent can attack the volley. Too short and you may not clear the net. Too fast and the ball arrives at a comfortable volley height. The shot works because of its trajectory and placement, not because it is merely soft.

## When should you try one?

A chiquita is most useful when you are balanced enough to control the ball and the net player is not so close that the target disappears. It can be particularly effective after you have shown several lobs and the opponents are thinking about moving backwards.

A drop shot is a more situational attacking choice. Look for opponents who have been pushed deep and a ball you can control comfortably without disguising a desperate defensive shot as touch.

## Do not force either shot

If you are stretched, late or defending heavy pressure, a high-percentage lob or controlled drive may be the better choice. Soft shots need control. They are not automatically safer just because they are slower.

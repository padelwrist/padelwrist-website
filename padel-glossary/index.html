Padel glossary

The padel words people use as if everyone already knows them.

Some are official scoring terms, some describe shots, and some belong to social formats. Here is the useful version, in plain English.

Scoring and match terms

Advantage

The point after deuce in traditional scoring. A pair with advantage wins the game if it wins the next point; losing it returns the score to deuce.

Break point

A point where the receiving pair can win the game and therefore break the opponents' service game.

Deuce

The score at 40-40. What happens next depends on the chosen format: advantage, golden point or another approved method.

Game

A unit within a set. In traditional scoring, points progress through love, 15, 30 and 40.

Golden Point

A no-advantage deciding point played at deuce. The receiving pair chooses the receiving side and the winner takes the game.

Love

Zero in traditional point scoring.

Match point

A point which, if won by the leading pair, ends the match.

Set point

A point which, if won by the leading pair, wins the set.

Star Point

A 2026 FIP scoring option that allows limited advantage play before a deciding point is used at the third deuce.

Super tie-break

A tie-break played to ten points by two, commonly used as an agreed replacement for a deciding set.

Tie-break

A numerical point game, normally first to seven by two, used to decide a set that reaches 6-6.

Social-format terms

Americano

A social rotation format in which players commonly change partners and collect individual points across fixed-point rounds. Exact rules vary by organiser.

Fixed Points

Scoring where every rally counts as one point rather than using 15, 30 and 40.

Mexicano

A social rotation format similar to Americano, but later pairings are commonly influenced by the current ranking so players performing at similar levels meet more often.

Common shot and court terms

Bandeja

A controlled overhead, usually played with slice, used to maintain net position rather than simply trying to hit a winner.

Bajada

An attacking shot played after the ball rebounds from the back glass, often struck from a higher contact point as the player moves forward.

Chiquita

A soft, low shot played towards the opponents' feet, often used to create an opportunity to move towards the net.

Lob

A high shot over the opponents intended to push them away from the net and create space to take the attacking position.

Netted serve / let

A serve that touches the net and still lands correctly may be replayed under the relevant let rule rather than counted as a point.

Víbora

An aggressive sliced overhead, typically faster and more attacking than a bandeja, designed to keep the ball low after the rebound.

Useful distinctions

A tie-break is not the same as a super tie-break. Golden point is not the same as advantage. And Fixed Points describes a way of counting rallies, not a complete tournament format on its own.

Those distinctions matter when configuring a match or trying to work out why two clubs appear to use different “padel rules”.

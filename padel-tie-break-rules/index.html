Padel tie-break rules.

At 6-6, the familiar 15, 30 and 40 disappears. A standard tie-break counts every rally as one point and normally continues until one pair reaches at least seven with a two-point lead.

Reviewed against the current International Padel Federation Rules of Padel.

How do you win?

First to seven points with a two-point margin. 7-5 ends the tie-break; 7-6 does not. At 6-6, play continues until somebody leads by two: 8-6, 9-7, 10-8 and so on.

Winning the standard tie-break normally records the set as 7-6.

Who serves first?

The player whose turn it is serves the opening point from the right. Service then passes to the other pair for two points, before continuing in two-point blocks through the service order already established in the set.

The easiest memory aid is 1-2-2-2: one point for the opening server, then two points per server thereafter.

When do you change ends?

Players change ends after every six total points in the tie-break: after 6, 12, 18 and so on.

What happens in the next set?

The pair that did not begin serving the tie-break starts serving the following set. As usual, a pair can choose its internal serving order again at the start of a new set.

What is a super tie-break?

A super tie-break is commonly used as an agreed replacement for a deciding set. It is usually played to ten points with a two-point margin. That is different from a standard seven-point tie-break used to settle a set at 6-6.

International Padel Federation official documents

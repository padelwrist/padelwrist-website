# A good lob does more than buy time. It can change who owns the net.

The lob is one of padel's most important tactical shots because it turns a low defensive contact into a problem above the opponents' heads. The key is choosing it from a position where you can control enough height and depth.

## Lob when the opponents control the net

If both opponents are settled at the net, driving through them is only one option. A deep lob forces them to move backwards and play an overhead or let the ball bounce, creating the opportunity for your pair to advance.

## Lob from balance, not panic

The worst lobs are often attempted while falling backwards or reaching below the knees. From that position, the ball tends to finish short and becomes an easy smash.

If you are under heavy pressure, first play a ball that restores balance. The right shot can still be a lob, but safety and height matter more than ambition.

## Depth matters more than dramatic height

A very high lob that lands near the service line gives the net player plenty of time to move under it. A more useful lob lands deep enough to force genuine retreat.

Height gives you margin over the racket; depth is what makes the opponents give up position.

## Move after a successful lob

If both opponents turn and retreat, do not stay at the back admiring the shot. Move forward with your partner. The tactical value of the lob is realised by taking the space it creates.

## When should you not lob?

If the opponents are already deep, a lob may simply give them an easy ball. If you have a high contact at the net, giving the ball back upwards may surrender your attacking position. And if the conditions make high balls difficult to control, use extra margin.

## Offensive and defensive lobs

A defensive lob prioritises time and safety when you are under pressure. An offensive lob is played from a more balanced position with enough depth to push the opponents away from the net and let you advance.

Both are useful. The mistake is trying to play an aggressive lob from a defensive body position.

## Where should you aim?

The middle can create hesitation over which opponent should take the overhead. Cross-court gives more diagonal length. Over a player's backhand side can be useful depending on handedness and court position.

Do not chase a perfect target before you can reliably land the ball deep.

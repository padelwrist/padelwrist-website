# Padel vs pickleball.

Both use solid rackets and are easier to start than many traditional racket sports. That is roughly where the similarity ends.

## The short answer

Padel is an enclosed-court sport built around glass rebounds and usually played as doubles. Pickleball uses a smaller open court, a plastic ball and a no-volley zone close to the net. They create very different rallies and movement patterns.

## Court

A standard padel court is 20m by 10m and enclosed by glass and mesh. Pickleball uses a badminton-sized rectangular court without rebound walls. The padel enclosure is part of the rally after the ball bounces.

## Racket and ball

Padel uses a solid perforated racket with a short handle and a pressurised rubber ball similar in appearance to a tennis ball. Pickleball uses a paddle and a lightweight hard plastic ball with holes. The contact feel and rebound are completely different.

## Serve

Both sports avoid the full overarm tennis serve, but their serving rules are not interchangeable. Padel requires the ball to bounce before an underarm strike into the diagonal service box. Pickleball has its own underhand or drop-serve rules and service sequence.

## Walls versus the kitchen

Padel's defining space is the glass: defenders can let balls rebound and continue the rally. Pickleball's defining spatial rule is the non-volley zone, commonly called the kitchen, which changes how players approach the net.

## Scoring

Padel normally uses tennis-style games and sets. Pickleball uses numerical rally/game scoring rules specific to pickleball. If you are moving between the sports, do not assume the score transfers just because both are racket games.

## Which is easier to start?

Both are accessible. Pickleball's small court and slower plastic ball can make basic exchanges approachable very quickly. Padel's underarm serve and walls also extend rallies, but understanding glass rebounds adds a new skill almost immediately.

## What feels different in an actual rally?

Padel asks you to think behind yourself. A ball that has passed your body may still be playable after the back glass, so retreating and reading rebounds becomes normal. Pickleball puts much more emphasis on controlling the space around the non-volley zone and handling a lightweight ball over shorter distances.

That difference is why a player can enjoy both sports without feeling as though one is simply a smaller version of the other. The movement, contact and tactical decisions are distinct from the first few sessions.

## Which one feels more like tennis?

Padel keeps tennis-style scoring and many familiar stroke concepts, but the walls and doubles positioning transform tactics. Pickleball has its own distinctive paddle feel, bounce and close-net patterns.

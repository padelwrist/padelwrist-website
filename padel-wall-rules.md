# Padel wall rules.

The easiest rule to remember is this: **floor first, wall second** . Once that clicks, most of the strange-looking rebounds make sense.

## The basic wall rule

When your opponents send the ball over the net, it must first land in your court. After that first bounce it can hit the glass or fence and remain in play. You must strike it before it bounces on the floor a second time.

## Padel ball in or out: the quick decisions

**Opponent's shot bounces in your court, then hits the glass:** in. The rally continues.

**Opponent's shot bounces in your court, then hits the metal fence:** in during a normal rally.

**Opponent's shot hits your glass or fence before the floor:** out. You win the point.

**Ball bounces twice on the floor before you return it:** point lost.

**Your return hits your own glass first, then crosses the net and lands in:** legal.

**Your return hits your own metal fence before crossing the net:** not a legal return.

**Serve lands in the correct box then hits glass:** it can remain legal.

**Serve lands in the correct box then hits the metal fence before the second bounce:** fault.

The important exception is the serve: glass and fence are treated differently there.

## What if the ball hits the wall before the floor?

If your opponents' shot hits your glass or fence before bouncing in your court, their shot is out and you win the point. The wall does not make a direct shot legal.

## Can you use your own glass?

Yes. You can intentionally hit the ball against your own glass and send it over the net. This is useful when the ball is awkward behind you or when a normal forward swing is difficult. The return still needs to cross the net and land legally in the opponents' court.

## Glass and fence are not identical on serve

During a normal rally, rebounds from both enclosure types can be legal after the floor bounce. On serve, the rule is more specific: if the served ball lands in the correct service box and then hits the metal fence, the serve is a fault. A rebound from the glass can remain legal.

For net-cord and replay situations, see [padel let rules](https://padelwrist.com/padel-let-rules/) .

## Can the ball hit two walls?

Yes, after the initial floor bounce. A ball can rebound from the back glass and side glass, or vice versa, and remain playable as long as there has not been a second floor bounce. Experienced defenders use those rebounds to buy time rather than rushing at the ball.

## From defending the glass to attacking from it

Once you are comfortable letting the wall return the ball, some high rebounds become attacking opportunities. A [bajada](https://padelwrist.com/how-to-play-bajada-padel/) uses a high back-glass rebound to play forwards with pressure rather than simply defending again.

Do not force it from every rebound. If the ball stays low or traps you close to the wall, create space and play the simpler defensive option.

## What beginners get wrong

**Hitting too early.** Players often attack a difficult ball before the back glass when letting it rebound would create a much easier contact.

**Watching the wall instead of the ball.** The useful cue is the bounce and trajectory, not staring at the glass.

**Assuming the fence always means out.** During a rally, a ball that bounces in the court and then reaches the fence can still be live.

## A simple first-session rule

If the ball is travelling behind you, do not automatically chase it. Let it bounce, give the glass a chance to send it back, then move into the space where the rebound will arrive. That single habit makes padel feel much less frantic.

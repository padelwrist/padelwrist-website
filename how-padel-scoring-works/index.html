How padel scoring works.

Padel uses the familiar tennis-style framework of points, games and sets. The part worth checking before a match is what happens at deuce, because advantage, deciding-point formats and the 2026 FIP Star Point system are not the same thing.

Reviewed against the current International Padel Federation rules and 2026 Star Point guidance.

The quick answer

In a standard match, points move from love to 15, 30 and 40. Games make up sets, and a match is commonly best of three sets. A set is normally won at six games with a two-game margin; at 6-6 a tie-break is normally used.

Point score: Love → 15 → 30 → 40 → game

Set: Normally first to 6 games by 2

At 6-6: Tie-break, normally first to 7 points by 2

Match: Commonly best of 3 sets

Points: love, 15, 30, 40 and game

The first point won makes the score 15, the second 30 and the third 40. Winning the next point can win the game, unless the chosen deuce format requires something else at 40-40.

“Love” simply means zero. So 30-love means the serving pair has won two points and the receiving pair has yet to win one.

What happens at deuce?

At 40-40 the game reaches deuce. This is where match formats can diverge, so it is worth agreeing the scoring system before the first serve.

Traditional advantage

A pair must win two consecutive points from deuce. The first gives advantage; winning the next wins the game. If the pair with advantage loses the next point, the score returns to deuce.

Golden Point / deciding point

Some formats replace advantage with a single deciding point at deuce. The exact receiving-side procedure depends on the rules being used, so club, league or tournament instructions take precedence.

Star Point in 2026

The International Padel Federation introduced Star Point for 2026 across Premier Padel, CUPRA FIP Tour, FIP Promises and FIP Beyond. It is designed as a middle ground between traditional advantage and immediate sudden death: limited advantage play is retained, with a deciding point used at the third deuce.

That means “professional padel uses Golden Point” is no longer a safe blanket statement in 2026. Check the event's current regulations.

Games make up a set

A set is normally won by the first pair to reach six games with a two-game lead. Scores such as 6-0, 6-2 and 6-4 therefore complete the set.

At 5-5, one pair can win 7-5 by taking the next two games. If it reaches 6-6, a tie-break is normally played.

How a tie-break works

Tie-breaks use ordinary numbers rather than 15, 30 and 40. The usual target is seven points with a two-point margin, so 7-5 ends it but 7-6 does not. Play continues until one pair leads by two.

The service order also changes within a tie-break. If that is the bit you tend to forget, see our dedicated padel tie-break rules guide.

Best of three sets

A full match is commonly best of three sets: the first pair to win two sets wins the match. A straight-sets result might be 6-3, 6-4; a three-set result might be 6-4, 3-6, 6-2.

Social sessions often shorten this because court bookings are time-limited. A one-set match, timed match or Fixed Points format is perfectly possible when everyone agrees the format before play.

Standard scoring and Fixed Points are different jobs

Standard scoring follows games and sets. Fixed Points counts every rally as one point toward a target. The latter is common in social formats such as Americano and Mexicano, although those formats also include pairing and rotation rules that vary by organiser.

Frequently asked questions

Is padel scoring exactly the same as tennis?

The core 15-30-40, game and set structure is tennis-style, but the deuce system can vary by event or agreed format.

How many games do you need to win a padel set?

Normally six with a two-game lead. At 6-6, a tie-break is normally used.

How many sets do you need to win?

Two sets in a best-of-three match.

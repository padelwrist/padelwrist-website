Point-based match formats

Fixed Points padel scoring: every rally counts as one.

Fixed Points strips away games and sets. Choose a point target or total, play the rallies, and count each one directly.

How Fixed Points works

Instead of love, 15, 30 and 40, the score moves 0-1, 1-1, 2-1 and so on. One rally produces one point. The round ends according to the agreed target or total.

That simplicity makes Fixed Points useful for organised social formats, timed sessions and training games where the traditional game-and-set structure is not the point of the exercise.

Fixed target vs fixed total

Two organisers can both say “32-point match” and mean different things. One may mean first pair to 32. Another may mean play exactly 32 rallies in total, so a final score could be 18-14. It is worth agreeing which version you mean before the first point.

Why Americano and Mexicano often use it

In social rotations, every player needs a useful score to carry into the table. A fixed number of rally points makes that easy because a 17-15 round gives both sides a meaningful contribution, rather than simply recording one match win and one loss.

It also helps organisers keep rounds to a more predictable length.

What about serving?

Point-based scoring does not automatically tell you how service should rotate. Some formats change server after a fixed block of points; others preserve a local club convention. The scoring format and the service format should therefore be treated as two separate decisions.

Fixed Points in PadelWrist

PadelWrist includes a generic Fixed Points mode for scoring an individual point-based match. It is deliberately not labelled as a complete Americano or Mexicano tournament manager because those formats also involve pairings, rotations and standings outside the score of one court.

That separation keeps the on-court job simple: record which side won the rally and keep the running point total visible.

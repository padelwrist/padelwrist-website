# How to Stop Getting Lobbed in Padel | PadelWrist

Tactics & positioning · Updated 27 August 2026

# You cannot stop every good lob. You can stop making them easy.

Getting lobbed is not always a positioning failure. A high-quality lob is meant to move you backwards. The aim is to reduce the easy lobs, recognise the good ones early and choose an overhead or retreat before you are off balance.

## Do not stand on top of the net

Closer is not automatically better. If you crowd the net while the opponent has time and balance, you leave a huge area behind you. Give yourself enough space to react and move backwards without surrendering your ability to volley.

## Read the opponent before they hit

A defender who is balanced underneath the ball has more lob options than one stretching low or wide. When you see a comfortable contact and an open racket face, be ready to turn and move rather than waiting to see the full flight.

## Make the lob difficult with your previous volley

A volley to the feet, middle or deep corner can force a lower, less balanced reply. A comfortable waist-height ball from the back is an invitation to lob. Preventing the lob starts with the quality of the shot before it.

## Move back sideways, not in a panic

Turn early and use controlled steps so you keep the ball in view. Backpedalling underneath a lob makes overhead timing harder and increases the chance of losing balance.

## Choose the overhead that matches the ball

A deep lob often calls for a bandeja. A more attackable lob may allow a víbora or smash. If the ball has beaten your overhead position entirely, retreat and defend instead of forcing a hero shot.

## Retreat with your partner

If one player is genuinely beaten by the lob, the pair's shape has changed. The partner should recognise whether staying at the net would leave the court split and move accordingly.

## Accept that some lobs win the net

The tactical mistake is not losing the net once. It is turning a good opponent lob into two errors by forcing an impossible overhead and leaving your partner exposed. Defend the next phase and look for the chance to lob back.

Watch what happens after you retreat. If you immediately rush forward again from a poor defensive ball, you often give the opponents an easier passing shot. Rebuild the point first, then move together when your own lob or low ball creates the opportunity.

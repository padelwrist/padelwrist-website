# How to Play With a Left-Handed Padel Partner | PadelWrist

Doubles tactics · Updated 27 August 2026

# A left-handed partner can put two forehands into the middle. That is useful only if you know who takes what.

The common right-hander-left-hander pairing often places the right-hander on the left and the left-hander on the right, so both players can use forehands and overheads through the centre. It creates attacking options but also requires clear ownership.

## Why the side combination is attractive

With a right-hander on the left and a left-hander on the right, both players have their forehand side towards the middle. High central balls can therefore be attacked by either player's stronger overhead side.

## Two forehands do not mean both should swing

The benefit disappears if every middle ball becomes an argument. Decide priorities based on ball height, direction, who is moving forwards and which player has the cleaner contact.

## Use communication earlier than usual

Because both players may feel entitled to the same central ball, calls such as “mine” and “yours” need to happen early. The player with the better view can also call opponent positions while the partner tracks a lob.

## Overhead coverage can become a strength

Central lobs are less likely to force a backhand overhead if each player can cover the middle with their dominant side. That can help a pair maintain the net, particularly when both players are comfortable with bandejas and víboras.

## The outside still matters

Do not become so focused on the middle advantage that you leave the line or corner undefended. Each player still needs to understand their own outside wall, defensive corner and wide passing lane.

## Return of serve is another reason to experiment

Some players feel much more comfortable returning from one side because of the ball path into their forehand or backhand. If the conventional right-hander-left-hander arrangement creates awkward returns for one player, test the alternative in practice rather than treating side choice as fixed.

The best partnership setup is the one that gives both players clear responsibilities and repeatable shots, not simply the one that looks most attacking on paper.

## Should the left-hander always play right?

No. Side choice also depends on level, personal strengths, return preferences and the partnership. The right-side convention is a tactical starting point, not a rule.
